Dear Editors,

I am pleased to submit the manuscript "Do Fine-Tuned Adapters Survive Foundation-Model Upgrades? Decision Support for Maintaining AI Assets Built on External Models" for consideration as a research article in Decision Support Systems.

Many organizations now build their AI capabilities by attaching small fine-tuned adapters to foundation models licensed from a provider. Every new model release forces a decision that most teams make by habit: retrain the adapter, keep the old one, or try a cheaper repair. The manuscript provides decision support for that choice. An optimal stopping model gives a closed-form retraining threshold and a price ceiling for any repair method, both driven by a quantity a team can measure, the share of the retraining benefit that the cheaper option keeps. We measure that quantity on 22 upgrades of open models across three tasks and calibrate the release rate from the release histories of six model families.

Three findings seem to us of direct use to practitioners. Routine continued pre-training updates left most of an adapter intact, so retraining paid off only for very valuable adapters. Moving from a base model to an instruction-tuned one often destroyed the adapter, and tracing one model family through its published post-training stages shows that supervised fine-tuning, not the later preference or reinforcement-learning updates, is the step that does the damage. Finally, the same upgrade affected adapters for different tasks very differently, so the decision has to be made adapter by adapter. The paper closes with a short procedure that turns these results into a routine a team can run with data it already has.

We believe the manuscript fits the journal's interest in decision support for managing information technology and in the economics of technology decisions. All code, configurations, and result files are publicly available, and the analysis can be reproduced on a single laptop GPU.

This manuscript has not been published and is not under consideration for publication elsewhere. The author has no competing interests to declare. Generative AI assistance used in preparing the work is disclosed in the manuscript as required by the journal's policy.

Thank you for considering this submission.
