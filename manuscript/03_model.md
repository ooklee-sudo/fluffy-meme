## 3. Model

A firm sends a stream of tasks to an AI agent. Each task is carried out in steps, where a step is one action of the agent, such as a tool call. A step costs $k$, and each error made in it costs a further $c$. After every step the task is completed with probability $q$, independently of everything else. At the start of each step the firm may instead hand the task to a human reviewer. This ends the agent's involvement and costs $H$, which covers the reviewer's time to take over and finish the task.

An error is a tool call that the environment rejects. Errors come in types $j = 1, \dots, J$, and the number of type-$j$ errors in a step is Poisson with mean $\mu_j$. Because the data allow us to tell them apart (Section 5), we give an error of type $j$ two possible consequences. With probability $\theta_{wj}$ it *derails* the task: the task still runs on and may be completed, but its output is wrong, and the firm then pays $F$ to detect and repair it. The firm does not observe derailment directly. With probability $\theta_{hj}$ the error instead leads the agent to *transfer* the task to a person on its own. The task then ends and costs $H$, exactly as if the firm had handed it off, except that the firm did not choose the timing. Errors are independent across steps and types, and we write $\tilde\theta_j = \theta_{wj} + \theta_{hj} - \theta_{wj}\theta_{hj}$ for the probability that an error has at least one of the two consequences.

The firm sees every error and its type, but not derailment, and it chooses when to hand off as a function of the history of errors. In Section 4.2 it also chooses the quality $d \in [0,1]$ of a check on finished outputs. A derailed output is caught with probability $d$, in which case the repair costs $F_d$; otherwise the wrong output reaches the customer and costs $F_u > F_d$. The expected cost of a derailed completion is therefore $F(d) = F_u - d\,(F_u - F_d)$, and the check itself costs $v(d)$, with $v$ convex and increasing. In Section 4.3 hand-offs queue for a limited number of reviewers.

The timing within a step is as follows. At the start of step $t$ the firm either hands off or lets the agent act. If the agent acts, the step cost is incurred and errors arrive; the agent may then transfer the task, and otherwise the task is completed with probability $q$. When a task is completed, the firm pays $F(d)$ times the probability that the task has been derailed. The firm minimizes expected total cost.

Three assumptions hold throughout. The firm observes errors and their types (A1). The parameters are known and constant (A2). Completion occurs with probability $q$ whatever the error history, so that errors affect the correctness of the output only through derailment (A3).

A note on terms: a *hand-off* is a transfer chosen by the firm, a *self-transfer* is a transfer chosen by the agent, and *escalation* covers both.
