Dear Editor-in-Chief,

Please consider the enclosed manuscript, "Your LLM Judge Measures Your Protocol: Lessons from Trying to Explain Stack Overflow's Decline," for publication as a feature article in *IEEE Software*.

Many engineering teams now compare coding assistants by letting one large language model grade another's answers. The article shows, using the decline of Stack Overflow as a case, how fragile such scores are. On the same questions, the grading rubric moved measured accuracy by up to 52 percentage points and one sentence of prompt placement moved it by 11 points, enough to reverse the ranking of models. Per-topic improvements between model versions did not replicate across prompt formats, and an apparently significant link between model capability and the loss of questions disappeared under placebo checks. The article closes with seven practices that teams can apply when they use LLM-graded accuracy to choose tools or track regressions.

The article is written for practitioners and is about 3,150 words plus one figure and two tables, with 13 references. All data, prompts, model outputs and scripts are openly available on Zenodo (doi: 10.5281/zenodo.23829331).

An earlier, longer research version of this work was submitted to *Empirical Software Engineering* (EMSE-D-26-01502) and was returned without review because of that journal's reviewing capacity. The present article has been substantially rewritten for a practitioner audience and is not under consideration elsewhere. Use of AI tools is disclosed in the Acknowledgment.
