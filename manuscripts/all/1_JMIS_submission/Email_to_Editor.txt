Please find attached my manuscript, "Delay Length Is Not Delay Cost: Loss Aversion, CIO Power, and the Timing of Switching in Generative AI Infrastructure," which I submit for consideration as a research article in the Journal of Management Information Systems.

The paper examines when firms move their generative AI systems from retrieval-augmented generation to fine-tuned models. Long delays in such switches are commonly read as large losses, and the paper shows that this reading can be wrong. In a behavioral real options model, a loss-averse CFO and a technology-focused CIO jointly set the switching threshold. Volatility then moves the length and the cost of the resulting delay in opposite directions: delay is longest in turbulent environments but destroys the largest share of option value in calm ones. Time-to-adopt measures can therefore misrank the value that firms lose, and governance of AI infrastructure decisions matters most when AI economics look stable. An exploratory survival analysis of job postings for S&P 1500 firms provides preliminary evidence on the timing mechanism.
