## 2. Hypothesis. What's the main question being asked or hypothesis being tested in this study?

Main question: Do vivid privacy-incident information and the format in which AI performance is presented distort professionals' choice between a cloud AI service (API) and a local small language model, even though neither changes the normatively relevant numbers?

Normative benchmark (risk-neutral; participants are told to decide as a risk-neutral manager and can compute it from the numbers shown). Per year, in thousands of dollars: the local model saves B in running cost but its extra errors cost E; the cloud API exposes personal data with probability λ per year, with loss L if exposure occurs. The net advantage of the local model is (B − E) + λL; it is optimal iff positive. The normative willingness to pay (WTP) to eliminate the API's exposure risk is λL. In every scenario B = 100 m, E = 180 m, L = 2,000 m with scenario scale m ∈ {0.6, 0.8, 1.0, 1.2}. For λ = 2% the net advantage is negative (API optimal; WTP* = 40 m); for λ = 6% it is positive (local optimal; WTP* = 120 m); break-even λ* = 4%. Thus the normative ln(WTP/L) rises by ln 3 = 1.099 from the low to the high λ. Neither the incident news item nor the performance format changes any of these numbers: the stated λ is described as based on industry-wide data on all reported incidents, and the performance difference is stated to be already included in B and E. Participants are not shown the product λL.

Primary confirmatory hypothesis:
- H2c (probability insensitivity). Participants who read a (fictional) data-exposure incident report are less sensitive to the stated exposure probability than participants who read a neutral news item: the within-person difference S = mean ln(WTP/L) at λ = 6% − mean ln(WTP/L) at λ = 2% is smaller in the incident group.

Secondary confirmatory hypotheses (Holm correction, family of two):
- H2a. The incident group has a higher mean ln(WTP/L) across the four scenarios.
- H3a. Presenting accuracy alone, rather than accuracy plus cost per correct answer, lowers the probability of choosing the local model.

Planned but not confirmatory: H2b (the incident effect on WTP is mediated by the excess of the participant's own estimated exposure probability over the stated one) and H3b (the performance-format effect on choice is mediated by perceived quality disadvantage of the local model).
Exploratory: H5, the incident effects on S (H2c) and on mean ln(WTP/L) (H2a) are smaller the stricter the organizational policy on sending sensitive data to external AI services (ordered from lenient to strict: allowed under general guidelines or no formal policy, S8 = 3 or 4; approval required for each case, S8 = 2; prohibited entirely, S8 = 1), and likewise decrease with lower personal discretion (P2), because salience-driven distortions should operate where formal rules leave discretion; H2d, the incident raises the likelihood that security and privacy policy is ranked among the top three sourcing factors; moderation of H3a by numeracy, AI experience and decision authority; the incident × performance-format interaction; and the relative support for two accounts of the incident effect, an upward shift of subjective probability (availability) versus a flatter response to stated probability (affect-driven probability insensitivity).

## 3. Dependent variable. Describe the key dependent variable(s) specifying how they will be measured.

In each of four scenarios: (DV1) choice between the local model and the cloud API (binary: local = 1); (DV2) WTP, the amount per year the participant would pay to eliminate the cloud API's data-exposure risk altogether, entered as a number in thousands of dollars between 0 and L/4 (entries above L/4 are set to L/4 and flagged; analysed as ln(WTP/L), with WTP = 0 replaced by 0.001 L). Derived: S as defined above, per participant (average over the two performance formats at each λ level).
Mediators and checks (measured after DV1 and DV2): the participant's own estimate of the yearly exposure probability in the scenario (0–100%); perceived quality disadvantage of the local model (1–7); a comprehension check per scenario (the stated exposure probability). Before the scenarios: the general estimated 12-month probability that a mid-sized company's personal data is exposed through an external AI vendor (0–100%). Covariates: job function, seniority, decision authority, budget authority, AI experience, numeracy, privacy concern, risk attitude, industry, perceived value of trade secrets and intellectual property (1–7), whether the organization handles classified or export-controlled information, personal discretion in sourcing (1–7). Organizational policy on sending sensitive data to external AI services (S8: prohibited entirely / approval required / allowed under guidelines / no formal policy / not sure) is asked before randomization. After the scenarios, participants select and rank their top three sourcing factors (running cost, accuracy, security and privacy policy, regulatory compliance, vendor dependence, in-house expertise).

## 4. Conditions. How many and which conditions will participants be assigned to?

Mixed design.
Between-subject: participants are randomly assigned (50:50) to read, before the scenarios, either a short fictional data-exposure incident report (an insurer's customer records exposed through its AI vendor) or a neutral fictional news item of equal length about companies comparing AI deployment options.
Within-subject 2×2: stated annual exposure probability λ (2% vs. 6%) × performance presentation (accuracy only vs. accuracy plus cost per correct answer). Each participant sees all four scenarios. Four business cases (customer inquiry classification, HR records, contract documents, insurance claims; scale m = 0.8, 0.6, 1.0, 1.2) are assigned to the four cells with a Latin square (four versions, equally likely); scenario order is randomized. Amounts are in USD, no country-specific rules are mentioned, and the scenario text states that the cost figures already include the performance difference.

## 5. Analyses. Specify exactly which analyses you will conduct to examine the main question/hypothesis.

- H2c (primary, α = .05, two-sided): Welch two-sample t-test of S between the incident and neutral groups. Effect size (Cohen's d) with 95% CI. Robustness: a linear mixed model of ln(WTP/L) with incident, λ level, their interaction, performance format, scenario position, business case and a participant random intercept; the incident × λ coefficient is the corresponding test.
- H2a and H3a (Holm family, overall α = .05, two-sided): H2a, Welch t-test of each participant's mean ln(WTP/L) across the four scenarios between groups. H3a, mixed-effects logistic regression: local choice ~ performance format + λ level + incident + scenario position + business case + (1 | participant); the performance-format coefficient is tested.
- Planned mediation: H2b, indirect effect of incident on mean ln(WTP/L) through the participant's mean excess probability (own estimate − stated λ, in percentage points), percentile bootstrap with 5,000 resamples at the participant level. H3b, multilevel mediation of performance format on local choice through perceived quality disadvantage, participant-level bootstrap (5,000). Reported with 95% CIs; not interpreted confirmatorily.
- Manipulation checks (reported, not part of the test family): the local model is chosen more often at λ = 6% than at 2% (λ coefficient in the logistic model); the incident group's general probability estimate differs from the neutral group's (Welch).
- H5 (exploratory): linear models of S and of mean ln(WTP/L) on incident, policy strictness (S8 ∈ {3, 4} coded 0, S8 = 2 coded 1, S8 = 1 coded 2; "not sure" omitted from this analysis only), and their interaction; the same with standardized personal discretion (P2) instead of S8. H2d: logistic regression of whether security and privacy policy is ranked in the top three on incident. Policy-prohibited respondents (S8 = 1) are included in all analyses and are also described separately: their number, share choosing the local model, WTP and S (descriptively only if fewer than 20).
- Exploratory analyses are labelled as such. Models are reported with and without covariates.

## 6. Outliers and Exclusions. Describe exactly how outliers will be defined and handled, and your precise rule(s) for excluding observations.

Exclusions are applied automatically before looking at the dependent variables. A participant is not eligible (and is screened out before the scenarios) unless: current job function is one of the listed IT/data/security/procurement/finance/general-management categories; their role in technology or vendor decisions is "final decision", "my recommendation effectively decides", or "member of the deciding group"; at least one of (level ≥ manager, can approve ≥ USD 10k of technology budget alone, or final/decisive role) holds; they have taken part in at least a discussion about adopting an AI/LLM service in the past two years; and their organization has at least 10 employees. Participants who took part in the authors' other study on AI sourcing are not eligible. Neither organizational policy nor industry is an eligibility or exclusion criterion: respondents who report that their organization prohibits sending such data to external AI services entirely (S8 = 1), and respondents in defense, intelligence or government organizations, are retained in all analyses, including the confirmatory ones (H2c, H2a, H3a). Because the manipulations are randomly assigned, retaining them does not bias the average effects, but if a sizeable share of respondents is bound by a rule rather than a trade-off, the pooled effects will be diluted. S8 is asked before randomization, so it cannot be affected by the manipulations. Sensitivity analyses repeat the confirmatory models excluding S8 = 1 respondents, and heterogeneity by policy strictness is examined in H5.
Among eligible respondents we exclude those who: fail either of two attention checks; answer incorrectly at least 2 of the 4 scenario comprehension checks; give job function or level at the end that differs from the screening answers; finish in less than one third of the median completion time; or give identical WTP and identical choices in all four scenarios. Entries for WTP above L/4 are set to L/4 (capping) and the number of capped entries is reported; no other outlier trimming is applied. We report the number excluded by each rule.

## 7. Sample Size. How many observations will be collected or what will determine sample size?

Target: 200 analysable participants (about 100 per incident group). We start by recruiting about 250 eligible-panel participants (assuming ~20% quality exclusions) and recruit additional batches until 200 participants pass all exclusion rules, with a hard cap of 300 recruited participants. No interim analysis of hypotheses is run and no data-dependent stopping is used. If the cap is reached with fewer than 200 analysable participants, we analyse those available and report the shortfall. Justification: with 200 analysable participants (100 per group), a two-group comparison at α = .05 (two-sided) has power .80 for d = 0.40 and .69 for d = 0.35 (H2c); at α = .025 the power for d = 0.40 is .72 and the smallest effect detectable with power .80 is d = 0.44 (H2a); a paired comparison at α = .025 has power .90 for dz = 0.25 and .80 for dz = 0.22 (H3a). The effect sizes are assumptions. The study is powered mainly for medium effects, so a null result for H2c or H2a will be reported as inconclusive for smaller effects. Sample size is not increased after observing effect sizes.

## 8. Anything else you would like to pre-register?

- Participants: professionals recruited through Prolific with employment/job-role filters where available; the screening items are used to verify eligibility because filters are self-reported. No country filter is applied.
- Compensation: hourly-equivalent rate of about USD 12, the platform's recommended minimum (about 19 minutes). The study is approved by the Hanyang University IRB (HY-IRB-2026-11-033); the IRB office confirmed that this study needs no amendment or separate approval.
- Design choices: participants decide as risk-neutral managers (stated in the instructions). No instruction asks participants to ignore the incident report. The product λL is never displayed.
- Fallback rule for the willingness-to-pay measure (fixed now, applied to the pilot only): if in the pilot 30% or more of participants give identical WTP in all four scenarios or fail the comprehension check in two or more scenarios, the primary dependent variable for H2c becomes the choice-based sensitivity S_choice = P(local | λ = 6%) − P(local | λ = 2%) per participant, and WTP analyses become secondary.
- Accuracy and cost figures for the local model and the API shown in the scenarios come from our own measurements of quantized open models on a laptop GPU and a cloud API; the cost figures B, E and L are fixed design values independent of those measurements. The cost per correct answer shown for the local model is computed from measured energy and latency with a stated cost model (hardware USD 1,500 amortized over 3 years at 50% utilization, electricity USD 0.12 per kWh); this levelized cost (hardware amortization plus electricity) is the only cost basis shown to participants; the energy-only (marginal) cost for organizations that already own the hardware and a fixed-versus-variable break-even analysis, which excludes setup labour, maintenance and staff, are reported in the appendix, together with sensitivity to utilization (25% and 75%). Anonymized data, materials and analysis code will be shared.
- Limitations stated in advance: people in organizations under the strictest security rules (e.g., military or intelligence) may be rare in the panel; we retain any who respond and report their number, but the extreme of the confidentiality-dominant regime may be under-represented; hypothetical stakes, self-reported job role, country-neutral scenarios, a single study, risk-neutrality instruction, and limited power for exploratory analyses.
- Any deviation from this plan will be reported and labelled.
