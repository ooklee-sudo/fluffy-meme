# Korean Air and airline stocks around the 2026 Iran war (plan `ANALYSIS_PLAN.md`; `analysis_airlines.py`; `results_airlines.json`); OPEC+ days (plan `ANALYSIS_PLAN_OPEC.md`; `opec_days.csv`)
**Part 1: onset (CAR over first 5 trading days: Korea from 3 March, U.S./Japan from 2 March; market model; empirical p).** Korean Air **−21.5% (p < 0.01)**, Jeju Air −20.1% (p < 0.01), T'way −19.9% (p < 0.01), Jin Air −15.3% (p < 0.01) versus KOSPI −17.6%; ANA (Japan) −10.3% (p < 0.01); United −9.4% (p = 0.07), American −11.0% (p = 0.06), Delta −6.3% (p = 0.18), JETS ETF −8.2% (p < 0.01); Exxon −0.1% (+14.3% over 20 days). Over 20 days: Korean Air −10.0% (p = 0.04), Jeju −13.0%, T'way −12.9%, Delta +15.4% (recovered).
**Part 2: path since the 27 Feb close.** Korean Air **+6.9%** (trough −20.3% on 9 March, peak +13.9% on 30 September); Jeju Air −13.5% (trough −34.1% on 29 July); Jin Air −16.5%; **T'way −62.5% and still at its low (−63.1% on 13 October)**; United −2.9%, Delta +21.1%, American −4.4%, JETS −5.8%; ANA −16.0%; S&P 500 +13.5%, KOSPI −1.8%, Exxon +14.2%.
**Part 3: oil sensitivity (per 1% WTI move, previous U.S. session).** During the war, with the S&P 500 control: Korean Air −0.085 (p = 0.29), Jeju −0.039 (p = 0.53), Jin −0.067 (p = 0.31); pre-war Korean Air −0.075 (p = 0.40); change not significant (p = 0.93). Oil alone in the war period: Korean Air **−0.176 (p = 0.02)**, Jeju −0.136 (p = 0.03), Jin Air −0.167 (p < 0.01); ANA −0.147 (p < 0.01). U.S. airlines show no relation to oil (United −0.008, Delta −0.004). Korean and Japanese airlines move inversely with oil, but the relation is mostly the common market factor and was already present (smaller) before the war.
**Part 4: Trump-statement events (next Korean trading day, abnormal vs previous U.S. S&P 500).** Korean Air: mean |AR| 3.45% vs 2.49% on random days (p = 0.07); threats −0.99% (p = 0.16 for negative); call-offs/deals **+2.45% (p = 0.086 for positive)**. Largest: 9 Mar −8.3% (after the 6 Mar 'unconditional surrender'), 8 Apr +7.7% (ceasefire), 12 Jun +6.2% (strike cancelled), 23 Jun −4.1%, 9 Jul −5.1% (memorandum 'over'). United: p = 0.44 (magnitude), call-offs +1.84% (p = 0.14); Delta call-offs +2.10% (p = 0.098).
**OPEC+ days as a less endogenous oil shock.** Five Sunday meetings found (3 May, 7 June [scheduled, unverified], 2 Aug, 6 Sept, 4 Oct; increases of 188 kb/d or unchanged targets). WTI moved +4.1%, +0.7%, −5.0%, +1.6%, −1.8% on the reaction days: mean |change| 2.65% vs 3.26% on other days (permutation p = 0.62). The instrument is **weak**: during the Hormuz disruption OPEC+ announcements did not move oil more than ordinary days, and five observations would not identify an effect anyway. Korean reactions on those days were driven by other things (e.g., 8 June KOSPI −8.6%, 3 Aug SK hynix −9.2%).
**Reading.** Korean airlines were hit as hard as the Korean market at the onset (−15% to −22% in a week), recovered partially (Korean Air is above its pre-war price, T'way at a new low), and are the most sensitive to Trump call-offs/deals (+2.5% on average, p = 0.09) — consistent with their jet-fuel and route exposure. A clean causal estimate of oil's effect on Korean Air needs an exogenous oil shock; OPEC+ days are not strong enough with the data available.

## Addendum: chips as controls for the oil sensitivity (plan `ANALYSIS_PLAN_CHIPCTRL.md`; `analysis_chipctrl.py`; `results_chipctrl.json`)
Coefficient on the previous-session WTI change (per 1%), HAC 5 lags, 2 Jan 2025–6 Oct 2026; S0 oil only; S1 + previous S&P 500; S2 + previous SOX; S3 + same-day Korean chip factor (average of Samsung and SK hynix).
- **Korean Air, war period:** S0 −0.176 (p = 0.02, R² 0.05); S1 −0.085 (p = 0.29); S2 −0.078 (p = 0.32); **S3 −0.105 (p = 0.06, R² 0.30)**. Pre-war: −0.014 (S0), −0.075 (S1), −0.073 (S2, S3; p ≈ 0.4). The war-minus-pre difference is not significant in any specification (p = 0.20 to 0.76).
- **Jin Air:** S3 war −0.085 (p = 0.05; R² 0.28), pre −0.065. **Jeju Air:** S3 war −0.056 (p = 0.25), pre +0.011 (difference p = 0.21). **ANA (Japan):** S3 war −0.100 (p = 0.01), pre −0.032 (difference p = 0.16).
- **The chip factor soaks up noise, not the oil effect.** Adding it raises R² from about 0.10 to 0.19–0.30 and leaves the oil coefficient about the same as with only the U.S. controls (Korean Air −0.085 → −0.105, slightly stronger, with a tighter standard error); the chip factor itself is nearly independent of oil (regression of the chip factor on previous-session WTI: pre +0.10, p = 0.43; war −0.07, p = 0.48; R² < 0.01), as the author argued.
**Reading.** With the chips as a control, Korean airlines show an oil elasticity of about −0.09 to −0.11 on the next trading day (a 10% oil rise goes with about −1% for Korean Air, −0.9% for Jin Air; significant at the 5–6% level in the war period for those two, not for Jeju Air). The sensitivity was already of similar size before the war (−0.07 for Korean Air) and its increase is not significant, so the data do not show that the war changed the airlines' oil exposure; they show that oil, with oil variance twice as large, moved them more in absolute terms. Oil remains correlated with war news, so this is a conditional association, not a causal estimate.
