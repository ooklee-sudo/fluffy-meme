# Korean airlines' oil sensitivity with chips as controls: plan (fixed before computing; written 2026-10-14)

**Idea (the author's).** Chip prices move with their own drivers (AI demand, memory prices, export controls) largely independently of oil shocks; so chip returns can serve as a control for the Korean-market and global-technology factor, removing noise without removing the oil effect.
**Data.** Daily closes (local dates): Korean Air (003490.KS), Jeju Air (089590.KS), Jin Air (272450.KS), ANA (9202.T) for comparison; controls: S&P 500 (previous U.S. session), SOX (previous U.S. session), Samsung and SK hynix (same Korean date; chip factor = their average return), KOSPI; WTI (FRED, previous U.S. session; through 6 Oct 2026). Sample 2 Jan 2025–6 Oct 2026; war period from 2 March 2026.
**Specifications for each airline (OLS, HAC 5 lags).** (S0) r = a + b·oil; (S1) + previous S&P 500; (S2) + previous SOX; (S3) + same-day Korean chip factor. Report b (oil) with its p-value, R², for the pre-war period, the war period, and the interaction test (war − pre) in the pooled regression. Hypothesis: b < 0 and robust to adding the chip controls (if chips are independent of oil, the coefficient should not move much when chips are added; if it falls to zero, the earlier oil effect was a market/tech factor).
**Check.** First-stage independence: regression of the Korean chip factor on oil (previous session) in the war period; the oil coefficient and R² of S3's chip factor on oil.
**Limitations.** Same-day Korean chips share the Korean market's day-specific shocks (including the oil news that day), so S3 may absorb part of a true oil effect; oil is itself reacting to war news.
