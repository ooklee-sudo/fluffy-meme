# SNAP ("food stamp") participation and benefit costs around the March 2026 war onset (exploratory; `ANALYSIS_PLAN.md`, `analysis_snap.py`, `results_snap.json`)
Data: USDA FNS national monthly SNAP data, Oct 2022–June 2026 (as of 11 Sept 2026). "Spending" = federal benefit costs (issuance).
- **Year-over-year changes.** Participants: −10.0% (Feb 2026) → −11.1% (Mar) → −11.5% (Apr) → −12.3% (May) → −12.8% (Jun). Benefit costs: −9.5% → −11.2% → −12.1% → −12.7% → −13.0%. Average benefit per person: +0.6% (Feb) → −0.2%, −0.7%, −0.4%, −0.2% (essentially flat). Mean YoY Nov 2025–Feb 2026 vs Mar–Jun 2026: participants −8.7% → −11.9%; costs −8.7% → −12.3%; benefit per person +0.05% → −0.38%.
- **Break test at March 2026.** The change in YoY growth between the preceding four months and the March–June 2026 months is −3.2 pp for participants (13th of 26 comparable windows, i.e. middle), −3.6 pp for benefit costs (9th of 26) and −0.4 pp for benefit per person (12th of 26). Similar or larger steps occurred in 2025 (e.g., −6.2 pp in the window ending Oct 2025). The decline is a continuation of a trend that began around autumn 2025.
**Reading.** SNAP participation and benefit spending fell by ≈12–13% from a year earlier in March–June 2026, but the fall was already ≈9–10% before the war (Feb 2026) and decelerating changes in the trend are not unusual. The decline coincides with the 2025 law's work-requirement changes (reported, from search results: participants about 42.2 million in May 2025 to 36.6 million in May 2026); the war cannot be separated from them. The average benefit per person did not change, as expected (benefit formula, indexed each October). Counts are people on the rolls, not purchases; actual redemptions at stores and fuel-price effects on household need are not in this file.
