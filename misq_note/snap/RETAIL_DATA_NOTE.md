# SNAP retailer / store-level spending data: what exists (searched 2026-10-14)
**Public official data (annual, not monthly).** USDA FNS "SNAP Retailer Management Year End Summary" (dashboard FY2024 and earlier PDFs; authorizations, redemptions by retailer type and state) and CBPP's SNAP Retailers Database (FY2019 redemption by store type). Supermarkets and super stores redeem about 78–82% of SNAP benefits. These are fiscal-year totals, so they cannot show a break at March 2026.
**Other official files.** FNS Benefit Redemption Patterns (research report, FY2022 data via the ALERT system); SNAP Retailer Locator (store locations, not sales).
**Monthly or weekly retail spending is only available from private panels and trade reporting (secondary sources, not downloaded here).** Circana (as reported by The Shelby Report, FoodNavigator, July–August 2026): EBT funding expected about $10 billion lower in 2026 than 2025; participation down about 10% in March and about 14% by May 2026 vs a year earlier; SNAP-EBT baskets down from about 10.4% to 7.3% of retail food and beverage dollars; SNAP households' Q1 grocery/CPG spending about $43 billion, about $4.6 billion lower than a year earlier; surviving SNAP households spent about 1.1% more per household vs 4.3% for non-SNAP households. Numerator (via CStore Dive, Grocery Dive): weekly grocery spending of SNAP households fell 10% ($233 to $210) in October 2025 during the funding lapse; state purchase restrictions could remove about $830 million of soda/candy/energy-drink sales by end 2026. Grocery Outlet: November 2025 EBT-linked comparable sales about −8.2%.
**Not found:** any public monthly store-level redemption series, or any SNAP fast-food/restaurant spending series (SNAP generally cannot be used at restaurants except state Restaurant Meals Programs).
**Implication.** The 2026 decline in SNAP-funded grocery spending is documented by participation (FNS) and by retail panels, and both point to the policy changes of 2025, not the war. These panel numbers need verification against the Circana/Numerator originals (paywalled) before citation; the participation figures from Circana (-10% in March) differ slightly from FNS (-11.1%).
