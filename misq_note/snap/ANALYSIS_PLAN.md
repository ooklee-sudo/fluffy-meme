# SNAP participation and benefits before and after the March 2026 war onset: exploratory plan (written 2026-10-14 before computing monthly changes)

**Status.** Exploratory (small sample, one confounding policy). Disclosure: the annual-summary rows were visible in the first inspection (FY2026 so far: 38.2 million persons, $189.15 average monthly benefit per person; FY2025: 42.1 million persons, $189.36); no monthly value was examined.
**Question.** Did SNAP participation, benefit costs ("food stamp spending"), or the average benefit per person change after the war began (28 Feb 2026)?
**Data.** USDA FNS, SNAP national monthly data, "FY monthly" file (`snap-4fymonthly-9.xlsx`, data as of 11 Sept 2026): participation (persons, households), benefit costs (dollars), average monthly benefit per person and household, Oct 2022–June 2026. Benefit costs are federal benefit issuance, not store sales.
**Statistics.** (1) Year-over-year % change by month for persons, benefit costs, and average benefit per person; compare Mar–Jun 2026 with Nov 2025–Feb 2026 (the months after the work-requirement provisions began but before the war). (2) A break test: change in YoY growth between the mean of Nov 2025–Feb 2026 and the mean of Mar–Jun 2026, compared with the same change between all earlier pairs of four-month windows with the same spacing (rank). (3) Average benefit per person is a price-and-composition measure; it should not respond to participation losses per se.
**Interpretation rules.** A war effect on SNAP is not identifiable: the 2025 budget law (OBBBA) tightened work requirements (ages 55–64, parents of children 14–17), restricted noncitizen eligibility, and changed state cost-sharing, with the effect on enrolment starting in late 2025; the Thrifty Food Plan cost-of-living adjustment changes benefits each October; and the Oct–Nov 2025 shutdown delayed benefits. Only a change in the pattern of these trends at March 2026 coinciding with the war is informative, and even that cannot be attributed to the war.
