# Low-income consumers and fast food during the 2022–23 gasoline surge: evidence collected 2026-10-14

Sources were searched for 2022–23 specifically; where the page could be read, quotes are verbatim; items taken only from search summaries are marked.

**Gasoline burden, lower-income households (BofA Institute, via Fortune, 11 July 2022; quotes from the article, not the BofA PDF, which could not be found).** "lower-income households saw their spending on gas alone reach 9.8% of their overall card spending" in June 2022, "up from just 7.7% in February" (share of card spending, not of income). The BofA economist's note said airline, lodging and restaurant spending by lower-income households were "basically flat" compared with a year earlier in the first week of July 2022. (BofA's 2026 gas-affordability report, 28 April 2026, states the 2026 gasoline-to-income share for lower-income households "remains well below its previous high in 2022" — 4.2% of income in March 2026 — so the 2022 burden was larger than the 2026 burden by this measure; the 2022 figure is not given in the text I extracted.)
**Restaurant traffic, all consumers (NPD Group, reported by Talk Business & Politics, 2 August 2022).** Restaurant traffic fell 2% year over year in Q2 2022 (6% below 2019); quick-service restaurants, "82% of total restaurant visits, declined by 2% in the second quarter"; "They trade down to lower-priced items, cut back on the number of items ordered, or reduce restaurant visits altogether" (NPD advisor). No income split in the article.
**McDonald's (search summary of CNBC, 26 July 2022; page not retrievable, so not verified verbatim).** Executives said some low-income consumers were trading down to cheaper options in response to inflation, a trend that continued in Q2 2022; U.S. same-store sales +3.7% (a figure verified in the SEC release). The SEC release for 2022Q2 and 2022Q3 reports "positive guest counts" for the U.S. (`../oil_income/MCD_INCOME_EVIDENCE.md`).
**Not found.** No 2022–23 public data set that splits fast-food visits or sales by household income (BofA's 2022 Consumer Checkpoint PDFs were not located at the Institute's URL pattern; Placer.ai and Black Box 2022 pieces were not retrievable; Black Box's "$3.50/$3.80 gasoline threshold" traffic figures appear only as citations in 2026 articles).

**Combined with our own tests for 2022–23.**
- 13-chain comps: no unusual decline at the 2022Q2 gasoline peak (Δ = −1.7 pp, rank 4 of 12, p = 0.33) and a rebound in 2022Q3 (`../oi_tracker/CHAINS_2022_RESULTS.md`).
- McDonald's: Δ_2022Q2 = +0.2 pp, above its peers by +2.1 pp (`../oil_income/MCD_RESULTS.md`).
- OI card data: no differential effect of the gasoline surge on lowest- vs highest-income-ZIP food-service spending (`../oi_tracker/RESULTS.md`, state exposure `RESULTS_STATE2022.md`).
**Reading.** In 2022 low-income households did spend more on gasoline (9.8% vs 7.7% of card spending) and traffic at quick-service restaurants fell about 2% overall, but chain sales (which include price increases) did not fall and early-July restaurant spending of lower-income households was flat; no income-specific fast-food data exist for the episode in what we could access. The claim "low-income consumers visited fast-food chains less in the 2022–23 gasoline surge" is neither shown nor refuted here. A direct test would need traffic by income (Placer.ai/Black Box/BofA) for 2022–23, which are not freely available.

## Addendum: BofA newsroom releases, 2022 (read in full via fetch, 2026-10-14)
- **6 June 2022 (week ending 28 May 2022).** "Gasoline spending as a share of total card spending surged to 7.8% for the week ending May 28, up from 6.4% in February"; for "lower-income consumers (<$50k/year), average share-of-gas-spending rose to 9.5%." The release does not mention restaurants or discretionary spending; "spending on services like travel and entertainment remained strong" (economist).
- **6 April 2022 (March data).** "Lower-income consumers feel a greater squeeze": cardholders under $50k/year spent 4% more than a year before (30.4% above 2019), versus +11.8% for those above $125k (22.5% above 2019); "restaurant spending rose by 17%" in March year over year (no income split); gasoline "up almost 50% from a year ago".
- **8 December 2022 and 10 January 2023 (year-end releases).** Nothing about restaurants, gasoline or income-specific restaurant/discretionary spending (only: balances above 2019 across income cohorts; only those above $100k spending relatively more on credit cards; retail +3.7% vs services +10% for 2022).
- The 2022 Consumer Checkpoint PDFs are not retrievable at the Institute's URL patterns (tried both).
**Net.** The only quantified lower-income result from 2022 in primary BofA text is gasoline's rising share of card spending (9.5% in late May 2022 for <$50k households; 9.8% in June per the Fortune report of the BofA note) and weaker overall spending growth for <$50k households in March 2022 (+4% vs +11.8% for >$125k); BofA's 2022 releases do not provide fast-food or restaurant spending by income. The lower-income restaurant observation (flat YoY in early July 2022) remains the Fortune report of the BofA economist's note.
