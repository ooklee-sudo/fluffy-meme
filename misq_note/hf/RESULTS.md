# Intraday reaction of oil futures and U.S. stocks to Trump's Iran posts (plan `ANALYSIS_PLAN.md` with Amendment 1; `analysis_hf.py`, `analysis_hf2.py`; outputs `results_hf.json`, `results_hf2.json`, `results_hf_robust.json`, `post_reactions_oil_coded.csv`)
Hourly bars (Yahoo; CL=F, BZ=F, S&P 500, Nasdaq, SOX, NVDA, UAL, XOM, USD/KRW). Reaction = log change from the open of the hourly bar containing the post to the close of the next bar. Null = the same two-hour change in all other bars since 2 March with no keyword-selected post within ±2 h (WTI n = 3,084; equities n = 774). 5,000 permutations.
**First run (pre-registered keyword classes) was misleading.** 'Threat' (N) posts were followed by WTI −0.73% (p < 0.001) and S&P +0.24%, SOX +0.77%; this was because class N included the principal de-escalation posts (hold-off of strikes on 7 April, 'very good and productive conversations' on 23 March, 11 June). Blind coding of all 191 keyword-selected posts (text only; ESC 63, DEESC 57, MIXED 12, OTHER 59) fixed this (Amendment 1; exploratory).
**Re-run with blind-coded classes (after the 3-hour cluster filter: 45 escalation, 49 de-escalation, 12 mixed posts).**
- **De-escalation posts (n = 33 with WTI bars): WTI −1.29% in the next 1–2 hours (p < 0.001; 73% negative; null sd 1.15%); Brent −1.21% (p < 0.001); USD/KRW −0.14% (won stronger; p < 0.001); S&P 500 +0.23% (p = 0.006; n = 14), Nasdaq +0.20% (p = 0.09); SOX +0.23% and NVDA +0.10% (not significant); XOM −0.02%; UAL +0.40% (p = 0.13).** The mean is driven partly by two posts (7 April 22:32Z: −12.0%; 23 March 11:23Z: −9.8%); without them WTI still falls 0.67% (p = 0.002; median −0.30%). Posts outside U.S. equity hours (overnight, thin markets) show larger oil moves (−2.25%, n = 16) than posts during U.S. hours (−0.39%, n = 17).
- **Escalation posts (n = 28): WTI −0.02% (p = 0.92), Brent +0.24% (p = 0.36), S&P +0.05%, Nasdaq +0.08%, SOX +0.22%, NVDA +0.26%, UAL +0.01%; none significant**; magnitude of the WTI move is not different from ordinary hours (|move| 0.98% vs null; p = 0.11), Brent's |move| is larger (1.53%, p = 0.01).
- **Mixed posts (n = 10):** WTI +0.30% (p = 0.38).
**Reading.** Oil reacts to Trump's de-escalation announcements (down about 1.3% on average, and up to 10–12% for the ceasefire/hold-off posts) but not to his threats; the asymmetry fits a market that treats threats as noise (many are repeated) and peace announcements as new information about Hormuz supply. Chips do not move significantly with these posts at the hourly horizon (SOX +0.2%, NVDA +0.1% on de-escalation), consistent with their independence of oil. The S&P 500 rises about 0.2% after de-escalation posts (p = 0.006). These are associations in a small number of events (14 equity-hour events per class); posting times are not random (posts also respond to developments), and exact post-time within the bar is not used. Dollar/won gains about 0.14% on de-escalation posts, consistent with the oil–won link found before.
**Implication for the previous question.** The de-escalation posts are a usable oil-price shock at the hourly horizon (first stage: WTI −1.3%, p < 0.001) that is independent of chips' own drivers; Korean airlines/chips could be linked to these via next-Korean-session returns (not yet done).
