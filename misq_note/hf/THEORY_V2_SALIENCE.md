# Theory v2 (salience) and confirmatory plan on hold-out topics (pre-registration; written 2026-10-14 before any Fed/China post is coded and before any return on them is computed)

**Why v2.** In `RESULTS_PAPER.md` the 'binding-constraint' theory (P1–P4) was supported only for oil de-escalation; for tariffs the escalation response was larger when tariffs were already high; learning (P2) and verifiability (P3) were not significant. The new model is a revision after seeing those in-sample results, so it must be tested on data not used to build it.
**Model (salience).** A post about a constraint K (oil supply, tariffs, interest rates, US–China restrictions) carries a signal s about future K. Investors process the post only to the extent they pay attention to the constraint: attention A_t ∈ [0,1] depends on how salient the constraint is (how much it has recently been in the news, i.e., the intensity of the posting process and the volatility of the related asset), as in rational-inattention models. The price impact of a post of direction d is ΔP = −β · A_t · c(type) · d, where c(type) is the credibility weight. Attention is increasing in recent posting intensity of the Poisson–Gamma process (higher λ over the past week ⇒ the constraint is 'live') and is high when K is at an extreme (war, tariffs after Liberation Day). Habituation (P2 of v1) is the competing force: within a short cluster the marginal informativeness of a repeated post falls. Hence the net effect of recent posting intensity on a post's impact is positive for a weekly horizon (attention) and non-positive for a short horizon (repetition): the model predicts a positive coefficient on 7-day intensity holding the 24-hour count fixed.
**Predictions (fixed now; confirmatory on hold-out topics, exploratory in-sample).**
- **S1 (attention):** the standardized directional price impact e_std increases with log(1 + n7), where n7 = number of same-topic coded signal posts in the previous 7 days, controlling for the number in the previous 24 hours and topic fixed effects. Confirmatory test: pooled hold-out topics (Fed, China), one-sided, coefficient > 0, α = 0.05. In-sample (oil, tariffs) shown as exploratory.
- **S2 (symmetry in direction given attention):** conditional on A_t (n7) and type, escalation and de-escalation impacts do not differ in magnitude (|e| equal). Test: coefficient on a de-escalation dummy in the pooled regression (hold-out) = 0 (two-sided; equivalence margin ±0.25 standardized units).
- **S3 (credibility):** the ACTION–RHETORIC difference in e_std is positive (pooled across all four topics, one-sided).
- **S4 (placebo):** posts coded OTHER have standardized impact not increasing in n7; pre-post-window moves are unrelated to the post's direction.
**Standardization.** e = reaction signed in the expected direction (as in `analysis_paper.py`; for Fed: easing posts expect ZN up, USD index down, ES up; tightening posts the opposite; for China: de-escalation expects USD/CNH down and ES up); e_std = e / σ, with σ the standard deviation of the asset's two-hour change over the previous 300 hourly bars (so that higher-volatility regimes do not mechanically generate larger impacts).
**Hold-out topics and assets.** Fed/monetary-policy posts (keywords `fed|powell|federal reserve|interest rate|rate cut|rates lower|too late|jerome|fomc`; primary asset 10-year Treasury futures ZN=F, secondary DXY, ES); US–China posts excluding posts already in the tariff sample (`china|chinese|xi jinping|beijing|yuan|rare earth|chip export`; primary asset USD/CNH, secondary ES). 14 Oct 2024–13 Oct 2026. Crypto posts are too few (29) and are dropped. Coding: two independent model instances (direction: ESC/DEESC/MIXED/OTHER with topic-specific definitions; type: ACTION/RHETORIC); primary labels = both agree; cluster filter 3 h; permutation inference where applicable; HC1 for regressions.
**Other pre-specified robustness.** (i) Other assets for the oil and tariff samples: ZN, DXY, CNH, gold (secondary; Holm over the four assets); (ii) a 5-minute subsample (posts from 5 Aug to 13 Oct 2026): reaction from the post time to +60 minutes with exact timestamps (WTI, Brent, ES); (iii) a daily-data extension to 2022–2024 posts (Russia/Ukraine oil posts and tariff/China posts) using S&P 500 and WTI daily returns on the post day and the next day (exploratory, low resolution); (iv) the human-coded 150-post subsample (two human coders; sheets distributed) to confirm validity when returned — not run by the model.
**Decision rule.** The salience model is regarded as supported if S1 is significant in the hold-out and S2 equivalence holds; if S1 fails in the hold-out, the paper reports the descriptive findings (state-dependent oil response; reliable coding pipeline) without the general theory.
**Limitations.** Salience proxies (posting intensity) are themselves influenced by events; model coders; hourly bars; small cells; multiple testing across secondary assets (Holm).
