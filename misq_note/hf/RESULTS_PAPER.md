# Unified design: tests of the pre-registered predictions P1–P4 (plan `THEORY_AND_PAPER_PLAN.md`; `analysis_paper.py`; `results_paper.json`; `paper_signal_posts.csv`)
Sample: 857 post-topic rows (428 oil/geopolitics, 429 tariff/trade; 42 posts appear in both), each coded by two independent model instances for direction (ESC/DEESC/MIXED/OTHER) and type (ACTION/RHETORIC). **Primary labels = posts on which both coders agree** (decided before looking at returns); 3-hour same-topic cluster filter → 169 oil and 100 tariff signal posts. Reaction = log change from the open of the hourly bar containing the post to the close of the next bar (oil: WTI; tariffs: E-mini S&P 500 futures, nearly 24 h). 'Directional effect' e = reaction signed so that positive = the expected direction (oil: ESC up, DEESC down; ES: ESC down, DEESC up). Regime 'high K' = oil: from the war (1 March 2026), tariffs: from 3 April 2025 (after 'Liberation Day'). Permutation p (5,000).
**Coding reliability.** Cohen's kappa between coders: direction 0.86 (oil 0.84, tariffs 0.86; agreement 91%), type 0.91 (on posts both coded ESC/DEESC; agreement 95%).
**P1 (binding constraint / state dependence).**
- Oil, de-escalation: directional effect 1.40% (high K, n = 31) vs 0.01% (low K, n = 24); difference 1.39 pp, **p = 0.014 (Holm-adjusted over the four primary tests: 0.056)**. Supported (WTI falls 1.4% after de-escalation posts during the war; nothing before).
- Oil, escalation (predicted larger when K is low): 0.15% (low, n = 29) vs 0.10% (high, n = 28), difference 0.05, p = 0.86. **Not supported.**
- Tariffs, escalation (predicted larger before Liberation Day, when K is low): ES −0.07% (low, n = 18) vs −0.35% (high, n = 24); difference −0.28 pp (permutation p = 0.09) — **opposite to the prediction**: tariff-escalation posts moved the market more after Liberation Day than before.
- Tariffs, de-escalation (predicted larger when K is high): +0.17% (high, n = 30) vs −0.04% (low, n = **3**); difference 0.21, p = 0.36 — direction as predicted but the pre-period has only 3 de-escalation posts (too few to test).
**P2 (learning/habituation).** Directional effect on the number of same-topic signal posts in the previous 24 hours: −0.08 per post (p = 0.63); on log(1+hours since the last such post): +0.14 (p = 0.16) (n = 187). Signs as predicted, **not significant**.
**P3 (verifiability premium).** ACTION minus RHETORIC directional effect: oil +0.56 pp (0.77% vs 0.21%, n = 47/60, p = 0.12); tariffs −0.08 pp (p = 0.51); pooled +0.30 pp (p = 0.20). **Not significant**; right sign for oil only.
**P4 (no unconditional asymmetry).** Consistent with the cell means: in oil the de-escalation response in the war dominates all other cells; in tariffs escalation dominates (post Liberation Day).
**Placebos.** Pre-post-window (two hours before the post) directional move: oil +0.14%, tariffs −0.07% (small, no systematic pre-trend). 'OTHER' posts (both coders agree) have smaller absolute reactions than signal posts (oil 0.71% vs 1.00%; tariffs 0.24% vs 0.34%): signal posts carry more market information than keyword-matched non-signal posts.
**Reading.** The theory is **partly supported**: the oil result is strongly state-dependent in the predicted way for de-escalation (the largest, cleanest effect) but the escalation half of P1, the learning prediction (P2) and the verifiability premium (P3) are not supported at conventional levels, and the tariff test of P1 contradicts the predicted direction for escalation (and has too few de-escalation observations before April 2025 to test the other half). A defensible paper cannot yet claim the binding-constraint principle in general; it can claim (i) a reliable LLM-coding pipeline (kappa 0.84–0.91), (ii) a robust, state-dependent oil response to de-escalation, (iii) evidence that the tariff response is larger when tariffs are already high (a different state-dependence: reaction increases with the level of the constraint), which suggests rethinking P1 (market reaction to announcements about a constraint scales with how salient/binding it already is, for escalation as well). Not done in this round: 5-minute subsample robustness, human-coded subsample, ZN/DXY/CNH/gold outcomes, Holm correction over all secondary outcomes.
