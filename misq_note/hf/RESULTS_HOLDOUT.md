# Confirmatory test of the salience model on hold-out topics (pre-registration `THEORY_V2_SALIENCE.md`; `analysis_holdout.py`; `results_holdout.json`; `holdout_signal_posts.csv`; robustness `analysis_robust.py`, `results_robust.json`, `fivemin_O.csv`, `fivemin_T.csv`)
Hold-out topics: Fed/monetary policy (150 posts; primary asset 10-year Treasury futures ZN=F) and non-tariff US–China posts (150; primary asset USD/CNH), 14 Oct 2024–13 Oct 2026; two independent model coders per post (kappa direction: Fed 0.92, China 0.87; in-sample oil 0.84, tariffs 0.86); primary labels = coders agree; 3-hour cluster filter → 55 Fed and 48 China signal posts (Fed: all easing/de-escalation, no tightening posts; China: 38 de-escalation, 10 escalation). Standardized directional impact e_std = e / (sd of two-hour changes over the previous 300 hourly bars).
**Descriptive.** Mean directional impact (percent): Fed easing posts −0.01 (e_std −0.16; i.e., no bond rally after posts calling for cuts or announcing nominations/measures that ease), China de-escalation +0.02 (e_std +0.25), China escalation +0.01 (e_std +0.15); in-sample for comparison: oil de-escalation +0.79 (e_std +0.58), oil escalation +0.12 (+0.25), tariff escalation +0.23 (+0.93), tariff de-escalation +0.15 (+0.65). The hold-out topics show essentially no hourly market reaction.
**S1 (attention; confirmatory, hold-out pooled): not supported.** Coefficient of log(1 + posts in the previous 7 days) on e_std = +0.045 (se 0.31; one-sided p = 0.44). In-sample (oil, tariffs; exploratory) +0.082 (p = 0.33); all four topics +0.071 (p = 0.33). The salience-through-posting-intensity channel is not detected.
**Habituation (n24, a secondary part of the model).** Coefficient of the number of same-topic signal posts in the previous 24 hours on e_std: hold-out −0.16 (p = 0.26); in-sample −0.28 (p = 0.07); all four topics **−0.24 (p = 0.036 two-sided)**, consistent with the repeat/habituation force (a post following others in the same day moves prices less) but not part of the confirmatory test and not corrected for multiple tests.
**S2 (direction symmetry, hold-out):** coefficient on the de-escalation dummy +0.19 (se 0.28; 95% CI [−0.36, +0.74]) — the CI is wider than the pre-specified equivalence margin of ±0.25, so symmetry can neither be confirmed nor rejected.
**S3 (credibility; ACTION vs RHETORIC):** coefficient on ACTION in e_std: hold-out +0.25 (p = 0.4 two-sided approximately; see `results_holdout.json`), all topics +0.24; not significant.
**S4 (placebos).** OTHER posts' |z| is unrelated to the 7-day intensity (coefficient +0.03, p = 0.66); the pre-post directional move is near zero (Fed −0.003, China −0.010, oil +0.137, tariffs −0.065).
**Robustness added (oil/tariffs).** Other assets (directional mapping: escalation = risk-off: ZN up, gold up, USD up, USD/CNH up): after oil de-escalation posts in the war: ZN +0.10% (Holm p = 0.004), DXY −0.11% (Holm p = 0.014), USD/CNH −0.04% (Holm p = 0.02), gold +0.32% (n.s.); no effects before the war or after oil escalation posts; tariff escalation posts after Liberation Day: USD/CNH +0.03% (Holm p = 0.08). 5-minute subsample (5 Aug–13 Oct 2026; windows from the post to +60 minutes): oil 9 posts (5 de-escalation: WTI −0.66% on average; 4 escalation: +0.16%; directional mean +0.44%, sign-flip p = 0.13); tariffs 5 posts, no effect: too few events.
**Decision under the pre-registered rule.** S1 failed in the hold-out, so the general salience model is **not supported**. What the data support: (i) a reliable LLM coding pipeline (kappa 0.84–0.92 on four topics); (ii) a large, state-dependent oil/dollar/yield response to de-escalation posts during the Hormuz closure (p ≤ 0.014 after Holm across assets); (iii) strong 'relevance' conditioning: posts on Fed policy and non-tariff China matters have no detectable hourly impact, while posts about tariffs (when in force) and about a blocked supply route do; (iv) tentative habituation (prior-24h posts reduce impact).
