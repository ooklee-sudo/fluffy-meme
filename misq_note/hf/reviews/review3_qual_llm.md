# MISQ Review - Reviewer 3 (interpretive/critical IS research; LLMs as annotators)

Manuscript: "When Does Executive Speech Move Markets? Platform-Mediated Announcements, Binding Constraints, and the Limits of General Theory" (MANUSCRIPT_MISQ.md)

## 1. Summary

The paper studies 4,838 posts from a head of state's platform account around the 2026 Hormuz closure. It pairs a pre-registered event study (de-escalation posts are followed by a 1.29% WTI fall; three general mechanisms are not confirmed) with an "interpretive analysis" of 136 posts. In that analysis two LLM instances open-code the posts blind to market data, the researchers reconcile the codebooks, and 20 cases are triangulated against market-wire coverage. The interpretive component yields five themes and four propositions, two of which fail held-out tests. The paper also claims a methodological contribution for "IS research that uses large language models as coders". My concerns lie mainly in the LLM-coding and interpretive parts, which the paper positions as central ("An interpretive analysis of 136 posts, triangulated against 20 market-wire cases"). As written, the two are not strong enough to support the claims made for them, and the integration with the quantitative component is thinner than the framing suggests.

## 2. Major concerns

**M1. Two instances of one model are not "independent coders", and the paper treats their agreement as validity.**
The abstract says "two independent model coders, working blind to market data". Section 3.3 says "Each post was classified by two independent model instances". Section 5.2 lists "independent instances working blind to outcomes" as a methodological contribution. Two instances of the same model family share weights, training data, and (judging from the coder files) largely the same prompt, so their errors are correlated. High kappa (0.84 to 0.92) shows reproducibility of a single instrument. It does not show that the instrument measures what a political-economy reader would call "de-escalation". The manuscript's own numbers show that agreement drops where the text is hard: kappa 0.62 for oil in the out-of-time sample and 0.37 for claims of agreement. Section 3.3 also notes that the keyword-based first run was "misleading". The LLM caught that error, but a shared-bias error would have gone through just as quietly.
The primary analysis keeps only posts "on which both instances agree". That rule selects the easy, prototypical posts and discards the ambiguous ones. The agreed-label sample is therefore a non-random subset, and the paper should say how many posts were dropped and whether the dropped posts differ in reaction size.
Why it matters: the headline result (n = 33 de-escalation posts) and every interpretive theme rest on labels whose validity is asserted, not demonstrated.
Remedy: (a) Stop calling the instances "independent". Call them "repeated runs" or "two prompt variants", and describe the shared-bias risk. (b) Use at least two different model families, ideally one open-weight, with different prompt wordings, and report cross-family kappa. (c) Report the share of posts dropped by the agreed-label rule, and re-estimate with the disagreements resolved by a human adjudicator and with them excluded. (d) Report prompt text, model identifiers and versions, temperature, and date of each run (see M7).

**M2. There is no human validation, and the infrastructure shows it could have been done.**
Section 3.3 says "Human validation of a 150-post subsample by two independent coders is under way [update before submission]". Appendix C is an empty placeholder. The repository contains human_coding_sheet_coderA.xlsx, human_coding_sheet_coderB.xlsx, human_coding_key.csv and eval_human.py, but no results_human_eval.json, so no human data have come back. Humans are also not part of the interpretive stage. ANALYSIS_PLAN_QUAL.md section 4 promises "peer debriefing: 사용자(연구자)가 코드북과 명제를 검토한 기록을 남긴다", meaning a record of the researcher reviewing the codebooks and propositions. No such record exists in qual/. reconciliation.md concludes that "두 분석자 모두 AI 인스턴스이며 인간 연구자의 검토가 필요하다" (both analysts are AI instances and human researcher review is needed). Section 5.4 of the manuscript concedes that "results should be incorporated before publication". I cannot review a manuscript whose validation is a promise.
Why it matters: LLM-as-annotator work is accepted when the annotator is benchmarked against humans on the same construct. The 150-post sample covers only the direction/type coding (the key file has columns model1/model2 and classes ESC/DEESC/OTHER). It does nothing for the 136-post interpretive coding, where no human has coded or reviewed anything.
Remedy: Complete the 150-post validation before resubmission and report human-human, human-model, and model-model kappa with per-class precision, using the existing eval_human.py. Re-estimate the headline result on human-agreed labels (the plan in Appendix C). For the interpretive stage, have at least one human analyst code a stratified 30 to 40 post subset from the blind input and reconcile with the model codebooks. Say who made each interpretive decision.

**M3. The interpretive component borrows the vocabulary of interpretive and critical research without the method.**
Myers and Klein (2011) is cited for "contextualization, reflexivity, and the consideration of multiple interpretations" (Section 2.3). The same sentence says "The study makes no emancipatory claim and takes no position on the content of the communications themselves". Critical research in Myers and Klein's sense is defined by the emancipatory, power-sensitive orientation that the paper explicitly disclaims. What remains is an inductive thematic coding exercise, which can be legitimate but should not be presented as critical IS research. Against the actual principles:
- Hermeneutic circle and interpretation of multiple voices: there is no engagement with how market participants (traders, analysts, desk notes) interpret the posts. The only voices are two LLMs and wire reports. The plan itself admits "구성원 확인(member checking)은 불가능" (member checking is impossible).
- Suspicion: the plan adopts a "suspicion principle" (section 1), but the manuscript never says what was suspected or what the analysis did with it. The plan then bars even reporting insider-trading allegations unless sourced.
- Reflexivity: the plan promises "성찰성: ... 정치적 민감성에 대한 연구자 입장을 별도 절에 기록" (a separate section on researcher stance and political sensitivity). The manuscript has no such section.
- Interpretation by the researcher: the plan says "해석 최종 판단은 인간 연구자" (final interpretive judgment by the human researcher). RESULTS_QUAL.md says instead "T1~T5의 해석은 연구자(AI 보조)의 해석이다" (the interpretation is by the researcher, AI-assisted). Who authored the themes is unclear.
- Section 3.6 invokes Gioia et al. (2013) "stepwise abstraction" and Lincoln and Guba trustworthiness, but a two-instance codebook crosswalk is not what those procedures describe (first-order concepts from informant language, second-order themes from researcher theory, aggregate dimensions with data structure).
Why it matters: MISQ readers of interpretive work will ask for a hermeneutic stance, a documented chain of evidence, and a position on what the interpretation is for. Here the interpretive findings are reduced to "hypothesis-generating" material and then tested statistically, which is a different epistemology (the paper moves from interpretivism to a post-positivist propositions-then-test logic without discussing the shift).
Remedy: Either (a) drop the claim to interpretive or critical research and describe the stage honestly as "LLM-assisted qualitative content analysis for hypothesis generation", cut the Myers and Klein framing, and cut Section 2.3 down; or (b) do the interpretive work properly. Option (b) means adding human interpretive analysis, a reflexivity section covering researcher position and the fact that the researchers had seen the reactions, an account of the hermeneutic move from parts to whole with a thick description of at least three cases, and market-participant voice (practitioner commentary, desk notes, or a small set of interviews). I recommend (a) unless the authors want to invest in (b).

**M4. The speech-act and Habermas framing is decorative.**
Section 2.1 uses Austin, Searle, and Habermas's three validity claims ("truth, sincerity, and normative rightness") to argue that audiences evaluate messages on dimensions that differ in how quickly they can be checked. I could not find any place where this is used.
- The coder files record a per-post speech_act field (for example "EXPRESSIVE", "MIXED"), and reconciliation.md gives "화행 범주 일치: 105/136 (77%)". Speech-act codes are never reported in the manuscript results and never enter a theme or proposition.
- Habermas's validity claims appear in the plan as "시장의 신뢰도 판단 차원" (dimensions of market credibility judgment). In the coder JSON they reduce to a free-text credibility_cue field. A search of RESULTS_QUAL.md and reconciliation.md finds no use of "validity claim" at all.
- The five themes (pressure and pause, concrete versus atmospheric, contested by counterparties, post as first announcement, other drivers) can be stated without any of this apparatus. T3 (counterparty denial) is the only one that touches Habermasian truth and sincerity claims, but it is described as an observed sequence, not analysed in those terms.
- The "declarative act" idea in Section 2.1 and theme T4 ("결정의 운반체") are the one substantive link. They are asserted by the authors, not coded: the "first announcement" classification (A/B/C) is a researcher reading of agent notes, not a speech-act code.
Why it matters: the paper uses the theory to look interdisciplinary, and the "A decision announcement is a declarative" claim is never tested against the speech-act coding the authors already have.
Remedy: Use it or drop it. A feasible use: take the existing speech_act field, report its distribution over the 61 posts with returns, and show whether declarative-type posts (decision announcements) have larger reactions than assertive and expressive ones. That tests the Section 2.1 claim directly with data in hand. If this is not done, delete Habermas and shorten Searle to a sentence.

**M5. The "sensitizing concepts" defense does not carry the weight placed on it.**
Section 2.3 says the concepts were used "only as sensitizing devices". The plan (section 1) says the same ("가설 아님"). The defense is sound for grounded theory but it cuts against the paper. If the concepts only sensitize, they cannot also supply the paper's conceptual claim in Section 2.1, where speech-act status "suggests" what markets evaluate. Nor can they justify the mixed-methods design in Section 5.2, which treats the interpretive stage as producing testable propositions. The concepts also shaped the coding instrument (speech_act and credibility_cue fields exist because of them), so they are not neutral lenses.
Remedy: State once, in one place, which role the concepts played in the instrument, in coding, and in interpretation. Report which codes were induced and which were imposed.

**M6. Sampling of the 20 triangulation cases is purposive but not what the manuscript says, and has structural problems.**
Section 3.6 says the 20 cases were chosen "by a pre-specified rule (the eight largest directional reactions, the six largest moves opposite to the post's direction, and six de-escalation posts with near-zero reaction)". The files do not quite match.
- The "top" group is selected by |e| (ANALYSIS_PLAN_QUAL.md amendment 2: "방향보정 반응 |e| 상위 8"), not by directional reaction. C03 has rx = -4.32 with direction ESC, so it moved opposite to the post but is in the top group. The "opposite" group (C09 to C14) therefore excludes the largest opposite move. The sample is also not "the six largest moves opposite" as stated.
- Two of the 20 cases are the same event as another case or are not announcements. C12 is an RT of C02 (triangulation.md: "C02 게시물의 재게시"). C16, C19 and C20 are links to articles. Four of 20 (the C group) carry no decision, so the effective n is 16 and the "flat" cell is half non-announcements.
- The selection is on the outcome (largest reactions, opposite-signed reactions, near-zero reactions), the opposite of independent sampling. It can show that cases with large reactions often involved announcements, but it cannot show that announcements cause large reactions, and it cannot yield a base rate. The manuscript's claim "many of the largest reactions occur when the post is itself the first public announcement" is therefore uninformative without the same classification for a comparison set of average and random posts.
- Section 4.4 says "Of the eight largest reactions, at least four ... fit that description", yet triangulation.md rates C08 as A with "이튿날 움직임 크기가 출처별로 2%~8%" (next-day move size varies from 2% to 8% by source) and posted after the close. The hourly reaction used for selection does not measure that.
- Plan section 3 step 4 required "같은 날 독립 매체 보도 2건 이상" (two or more independent same-day reports per case). triangulation.md admits that CNBC, CNN, Axios and others returned 403/429, that WebFetch gave "페이지 요약" (page summaries) so quotations are approximate, and that many oil reports depend on Bloomberg via Rigzone. C04 and C18 are only partly verified, and C06, C07, C08, C10 depend on indirect sources. The A/B/C coding is a single-researcher reading of agent notes.
Why it matters: the key interpretive claims (T4 "post as carrier of decision", T5 "multiple causes") are drawn from this 20-case set.
Remedy: Add a comparison sample of 20 random oil posts drawn from the same 106, classified with the same A/B/C scheme and the same blind procedure, so that the share of "first announcement" among large reactions can be compared with its share among small ones. Replace C12 with the next-ranked case, and re-run selection by the stated rule, not by |e|. Have a human code A/B/C independently and report agreement. Rely on archived primary sources (Reuters, Bloomberg terminal archive, exchange data) rather than search summaries.

**M7. Researcher bias from having seen the reactions is mitigated procedurally but not enough, and the manuscript under-discloses it.**
The plan states "연구자(AI 보조 포함)는 이미 이 게시물들의 정량 반응 결과를 알고 있다(RESULTS.md)" (the researchers, AI-assisted included, already know the reactions). Section 3.6 says the posts were coded "removed all labels and market information", and the 20 cases were researched "without revealing our reaction values to the researchers". The codebooks were frozen before linking, which is good practice. But:
- The coder agents are LLM instances with extensive pretraining on the 2026 events (if the model's knowledge covers them) or at least on the general pattern of this speaker's posts. Blindness to the market data is not blindness to the outcome; posts such as the 7 April ceasefire may be recognized from training data.
- The 20 cases were chosen by reaction size, which the researchers did know. The research agents were blind to the numbers but not to the case-selection logic implied by "top", "opposite" and "flat" groups, and agents given a case with a known post time and text will search for a cause, so finding one (T5: "every one of the 20 triangulated cases has other reported drivers") is nearly guaranteed. That finding has no discriminating power without a comparison set. Seven non-mentions out of 20 is similarly unreadable without a baseline.
- RESULTS_QUAL.md concedes "반응값을 본 뒤 패턴을 읽었다. ... 주제 서술은 연결 후에 확정되었다" (patterns were read after seeing the reactions; theme wording was fixed after linking). The manuscript does not say this. It says only "Their codebooks were reconciled and fixed before reactions were linked".
- The propositions were frozen after linking and triangulation but derived from them, so the in-sample motivation is circular. The manuscript's Q4 acknowledges this ("exploratory because the cases motivating it were in the sample"), but Q1 has the same problem and is not flagged.
Remedy: Add a reflexivity statement (who knew what, when, with dates tied to commits). Have an analyst who has not seen the quantitative results write the theme descriptions from the coded data. Test whether models can identify the post's date and outcome from the text alone (memorization probe). Report the comparison-sample results from M6.

**M8. The test of the interpretive propositions is too weak to support the abstract's wording, and the mixed-methods integration is shallow.**
The abstract says the interpretive analysis "generates propositions that are likewise not supported in held-out tests". Only two of four were tested (Q2 and Q3 are deferred). In Section 4.5 the Q1 test has a minimum detectable effect of 0.75 SD, and the original-posts mean is +0.03, meaning the original posts in the pseudo-out-of-sample show no reaction either (RESULTS_QUAL_TESTS.md: "ORIG 평균 자체가 0과 거의 같다... 구별할 대상이 없었다", the original posts themselves average about zero, so there was nothing to distinguish from re-posts). A null here is uninformative about whether the proposition is true. The test set is also a mixture of daily 2022 to 2024 reactions and hourly reactions on monetary-policy and China posts, topics where the manuscript elsewhere reports "essentially no hourly reaction to posts at all". Q4 is an in-sample measurement exercise. Calling both "not supported" suggests falsification where the correct statement is "untestable with the data available".
On integration: the quantitative and qualitative parts barely talk to each other.
- The quantitative section establishes a conditional response to de-escalation posts. The qualitative section concludes "the channel is hard to separate from the decisions it carries" (Section 4.4), which is closer to a restatement of the quantitative finding than an independent contribution. T1 (pressure then pause) is compatible with the quantitative asymmetry (de-escalation moves oil, escalation does not) but is not used to explain it.
- The 20 cases are drawn from the 106 oil posts used in the quantitative part, so the same data serve both strands. This is not triangulation in the sense of independent evidence.
- The propositions are tested by the same event-study machinery. The qualitative stage does not change any decision in the quantitative design, and neither strand has a role in the final theory beyond the boundary-condition statement.
- Many details of the qualitative strand appear in an unsupported form: the PAUSE_RELIEF median of 2.86 and THIRD_PARTY of 3.41 (n = 3) come from RESULTS_QUAL_linked.md, which says explicitly "검정이 아니다" (not a test), and T1's contrast between 0.33 and 2.86 rests on 13 and 7 posts of which two events carry most of the mean (the manuscript says the same of the war-period WTI mean).
Remedy: Reword the abstract to "two of four propositions were testable and were not supported; the tests had low power". Move Q1 to the "untestable" bucket, or build a proper test set (original versus re-posted posts within the oil war sample, with returns). State clearly what each strand contributes that the other cannot, and consider cutting the interpretive material to a short section and the methodological contribution to a transparency note. If it stays, use a joint display (cases by code by reaction) to show where interpretation changes the quantitative reading, for example by using T4 to redefine the signal (excluding RTs and links, as the authors themselves suggest in RESULTS_QUAL.md) and re-estimating the main result.

**M9. Reproducibility, ethics, and dual use are barely addressed.**
- The Declarations section says only "Large language models were used as classification instruments in the study and in drafting and analysis support. The journal's disclosure requirements should be checked". The manuscript gives no model names, versions, prompts, temperatures, or run dates. The qualitative coder files contain codebooks and outputs but not the prompt that produced them. LLM annotation is not reproducible without these, since hosted models are updated and deprecated. The repository DOI is a placeholder.
- The paper is about the market effect of a sitting head of state's posts on a live war. The plan acknowledges "정치적 민감성" but the manuscript has no ethics or positionality statement. At minimum it should address: (i) the risk that a trading rule "signal-detection rule on executive posts" (Section 5.3) functions as a dual-use trading tool and the "we make no claim about any trading behavior" disclaimer is not enough; (ii) the risk that posts used as corpus are quoted at length in a repository (the 2026 posts include insults and claims about named people); (iii) the potential for the findings to be read as evidence of market manipulation or insider trading by the speaker or those around him. The plan's own rule (allegations only when credibly reported) is sensible but absent from the paper.
- Pre-registration is cited as the paper's main credibility device, but the ledger in Appendix A is a list of repository files. A reviewer cannot verify that the plans predate analyses without timestamped, third-party-registered records (OSF or similar). The plan files also contain post hoc amendments (the "수정 기록" blocks), made after coding began, that change the sampling rule (amendment 1 replaced the stratified maximum-variation design with "all 106 plus 30 random") and the case selection (amendment 2).
- The plans mention outputs that are not in the repository: qual/codebook_v1.md and qual/triangulation_C*.md. The actual files are triangulation_A.json and triangulation_B.json, and the merged codebook is described inside reconciliation.md.
Remedy: Add a full LLM-use appendix (models, versions, prompts, parameters, dates, cost, seed handling, raw outputs deposited), a positionality and ethics statement, and a dual-use paragraph. Deposit plans in a timestamped registry, or say honestly that they are repository commits and give hashes.

## 3. Minor concerns

1. The sentence "Oil, which had risen from roughly $67 to $114.6 per barrel since the first U.S. trading day of the war, fell by double digits in the next session" opens with a dramatic narrative but the paper later says two events carry about half the mean. Use the median in the introduction.
2. Section 3.3 reports kappas for direction but not the confusion matrix or the proportion in the OTHER and MIXED classes. Kappa depends on prevalence, which is highly unbalanced here.
3. The out-of-time oil kappa of 0.62 is reported without discussion, yet this is the sample used for pseudo-out-of-sample tests of Q1.
4. "Five themes were retained" (Section 4.4) but the reconciliation lists six themes per analyst, and the theme-correspondence rule (">=50% core match") is applied by the researchers, not by an independent person. The statement "theme-level agreement on all six of one analyst's themes (some merged)" reads better than it is: analyst 2 has two themes with no counterpart, and several analyst 1 themes were mapped onto the same analyst 2 theme to reach 100%.
5. The post-level agreement in Section 4.4 comes from a crosswalk between two non-identical codebooks, built after the fact. Analyst 1 was limited to four codes per post (reconciliation.md "분석자 1은 게시물당 코드를 최대 4개로 제한해 ... 잘렸다"), which depresses agreement for multi-code posts and makes kappa comparisons across concepts unreliable. Both the kappa range and the "no tests" caveat belong in the text.
6. Theme T3 and Section 4.4 cite "the Iranian state denied that talks were taking place" and "oil rebounded the next session". These come from agent summaries of web pages that could not be fetched directly (triangulation.md). Cite primary sources and flag which are verified.
7. The two claims "(eight largest reactions) at least four" and "20 of 20 have other drivers" should carry numbers with a denominator and a comparison, as noted in M6.
8. Section 3.6 says "30 randomly drawn tariff posts", but no tariff post enters the 20 triangulated cases and the linked results table has n = 0 for TARIFF (RESULTS_QUAL_linked.md). The tariff posts add to the coded set without contributing to any theme or case.
9. The citation of Gioia et al. (2013) and Lincoln and Guba (1985) is formulaic; Myers and Klein (2011) is cited for principles that the paper declines to follow. Sandelowski, or work on LLM-assisted qualitative analysis (for instance the growing literature on LLMs as qualitative coders), would be more apt than critical-theory sources.
10. "Agreement-only" labels interact with the 3-hour same-topic exclusion and the permutation null; the manuscript should state how many posts survive each filter.
11. The reference list is flagged as unverified in the manuscript itself. Section 1 cites Tetlock 2007 as "the news-media precursor" and Bollen et al. 2011 for IS research; neither is an IS paper, and recent MISQ/ISR work on social media and markets is missing.

## 5. Recommendation: Major revision (leaning reject-and-resubmit for the interpretive component)

The quantitative core is carefully reported, and the commitment to publish null results and flag low power is commendable. The interpretive and LLM-coder parts, on which the paper rests a good deal of its novelty (the keywords, the "methodological contribution", Section 2.3, Section 4.4, Section 4.5, Section 5.2), do not meet the bar for either of the two traditions they invoke. Against LLM-annotation norms, there is no human validation, the "independence" is not independence, and the model and prompt provenance is missing. Against interpretive and critical standards, the study has no hermeneutic engagement, no reflexivity section, no market-participant voice, a disclaimed critical orientation, and a decorative theoretical frame. The mixed-methods claim is weaker still: the two strands use the same data, the qualitative sample is selected on the outcome, the "held-out tests" are underpowered nulls, and the qualitative work does not alter the quantitative design or conclusions.
I would be willing to see a revised version that (1) completes human validation and uses at least two model families; (2) either honestly relabels the qualitative stage as LLM-assisted content analysis for hypothesis generation, or adds real interpretive work with human analysts and practitioner voice; (3) tests the speech-act claim with the speech-act codes already collected, or removes the framing; (4) adds a random comparison sample for the triangulation; (5) provides a full LLM-use appendix plus ethics, positionality, and dual-use statements; and (6) rewrites the abstract and Section 5.2 so that they claim only what the evidence shows. Without these, the paper is a competent event study with an add-on that the interpretive-research community would not recognise as such. If the authors choose the relabeling route and cut the interpretive claims to a modest section, the remaining paper may fit better in a finance or computational social science outlet than in MISQ, whose contribution standard requires a stronger IS-theoretic link than the "boundary condition" in Section 5.2.
