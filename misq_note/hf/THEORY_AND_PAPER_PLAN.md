# One paper: information arrival, credibility and state-dependent price impact of presidential posts (theory, pre-registered predictions, sample expansion, identification, IS contribution; written 2026-10-14 BEFORE any tariff-sample return was examined)

**Working title.** "When do presidential posts move markets? Information arrival, credibility, and the binding-constraint principle." (Merges the review-count Poisson/LLM-measurement line of this project with the Iran-war market evidence.)

## 1. Theory (formal sketch; to be developed)
**Setup.** An asset's value depends on the state of a policy 'constraint' K ∈ [0,1] that restricts a flow relevant to the asset (K = share of oil supply blocked; K = average effective tariff burden). A post s_t about the constraint arrives according to a Poisson process with intensity λ_t (the posting rate; posts cluster, i.e. λ_t is itself random, Gamma-distributed: Poisson–Gamma, which our data support: dispersion index 350, inter-arrival CV 1.9). Each post carries a direction d ∈ {escalate (K↑), de-escalate (K↓)} and a type: ACTION (a decision already taken or a signed deal; verifiable) or RHETORIC (threat, warning, conditional statement, opinion; cheap talk). Investors hold a prior on K_{t+1}; a post updates it with weight = credibility c(type, history), which falls with the number of recent similar unfulfilled posts (Bayesian learning with a Beta prior on the 'follow-through' probability).
**Price impact.** ΔP = −β · c · [h(K, d)] where the sensitivity is the marginal change in expected value from moving K in the direction d. With a convex cost of the constraint (value loss L(K) increasing and convex), the marginal value of relaxing is higher when K is already high, and the marginal value of tightening is higher when K is low (diminishing damage near the cap, since much of the damage is priced).
**Propositions (testable predictions, fixed here).**
- **P1 (binding-constraint / state dependence).** The impact of de-escalation posts is increasing in K; the impact of escalation posts is decreasing in K (price impact ∝ K for de-escalation, ∝ (1−K) for escalation).
  - Oil: K is high in the war period (Hormuz closed, from 2 March 2026) and low before. Predicted and already observed (exploratory): de-escalation lowers WTI by 1.3% in the war vs 0.3% before; escalation raises WTI only when K is low (Russia/Venezuela sanctions: +0.4%, before the war), not in the war. **To be tested out of sample on tariffs:** let K = pre-/post-'Liberation Day' (2 April 2025) tariff regime. Predictions P1-T: before 2 April 2025, tariff-escalation posts lower U.S. equity futures (ES) more than tariff-de-escalation posts raise them; after 2 April 2025, de-escalation posts (deals, pauses, exemptions) raise ES more than escalation posts lower it. Formally: (ESC effect pre) < (ESC effect post) in absolute value, (DEESC effect post) > (DEESC effect pre).
- **P2 (learning / habituation).** Within the same topic, the impact of a post falls with the number of similar posts in the previous 24 hours and rises with the time since the last similar post (surprise/Poisson–Gamma): ΔP is decreasing in n_prev24 and increasing in log(1 + gap hours).
- **P3 (verifiability premium).** ACTION posts have larger impact than RHETORIC posts of the same direction (in absolute value), in both the oil and the tariff samples.
- **P4 (asymmetry is not universal).** There is no unconditional asymmetry between escalation and de-escalation impacts; the sign pattern is determined by K (P1). The pooled asymmetry found in the Iran war is a special case.
**Not predicted / exploratory.** Fed/interest-rate posts (149 matched posts; no clear K); crypto; effects on chips (expected ≈ 0 for oil-related posts).

## 2. Sample expansion (fixed now)
Posts from 14 Oct 2024 to 13 Oct 2026 (hourly futures available for two years).
- Oil sample (existing, 428 posts: W 191, A 65, B 172) — add the new 'type' coding.
- **Tariff/trade sample (new): 429 posts** matching `tariff|trade deal|trade war|reciprocal|import dut|trade agreement|trade talks|trade negotiat`, excluding text-free retweets; direction scheme: ESC = announces/threatens new or higher tariffs or trade restrictions, trade-war escalation; DEESC = announces/threatens lower tariffs, a pause/delay/exemption, a trade deal or progress toward one; MIXED; OTHER (e.g. boasts about tariff revenue, unrelated mentions).
- Assets (hourly bars, Yahoo): E-mini S&P 500 futures (ES=F, near 24h), S&P 500 (regular hours), USD index (DX-Y.NYB), offshore yuan (CNH=X), 10-year Treasury futures (ZN=F), gold (GC=F), WTI, Brent.
- Coding: two independent model instances per post (direction + type), no market information; human coding of a 150-post random subsample (sheets prepared) later.

## 3. Identification improvements (fixed now)
(a) **Timing:** primary reaction window is from the open of the hourly bar containing the post to the close of the next bar; an alternative (i) restricts to posts made when the relevant market is closed or thin versus open (reaction at the next open, 'overnight gap', with the pre-post trend controlled); (ii) 5-minute bars for the posts of the last 60 days (Aug–Oct 2026) as a robustness subsample with windows [−30, +60] minutes (exact post second). (b) **Pre-trend check:** return in the two hours before the post must be unrelated to the post's direction (placebo window). (c) **Placebo posts:** keyword-matched posts classified OTHER should show no systematic reaction. (d) **Reverse-causality control:** include the pre-post two-hour return and the previous day's return as covariates. (e) **Clustering/dependence:** one observation per 3-hour cluster; standard errors by permutation within topic-regime; multiple-testing control (Holm) over the pre-specified P1–P3 tests.
## 4. IS contribution (positioning, to be written)
(i) Social media as an information-arrival process whose count distribution (Poisson–Gamma) and content (LLM-coded direction/type) jointly determine price impact — the same count-model/LLM-measurement framework used for user-generated content in the earlier parts of this project; (ii) a measurement contribution: validated LLM stance coding (two model coders, human subsample, kappa) and a pre-registered intraday event-study protocol; (iii) managerial/policy implication: platform and trading-system designers need to condition on state (binding constraint) and verifiability, not just on sentiment.
## 5. Pre-registered tests (primary)
T1 (P1 oil): difference in WTI reactions to DEESC posts between war and pre-war, and to ESC posts; T2 (P1 tariffs): the same for ES with the 2 April 2025 break; T3 (P2): regression of the signed, direction-adjusted reaction on n_prev24 and log gap, pooled across topics with topic fixed effects; T4 (P3): ACTION vs RHETORIC difference. Primary outcomes: WTI (oil) and ES futures (tariffs); everything else secondary.
