# Korean airlines and oil, instrumented by de-escalation posts (plan `ANALYSIS_PLAN_IV.md`; `analysis_iv.py`; `results_iv.json`, `iv_sessions.csv`)
146 Korean sessions (2 March–13 Oct 2026) with complete hourly WTI data; 33 sessions contained a blind-coded de-escalation (DEESC) post, 29 an escalation (ESC) post, 4 both. Controls: previous-session S&P 500 and SOX returns, same-session Korean chip factor; HC1 standard errors.
**First stage is weak.** Mean WTI change over the Korean session window: −1.23% in DEESC sessions vs +0.25% in sessions without any post. Regression coefficient on DEESC: −1.53 pp (se 0.91, p = 0.09), first-stage F = 2.8 (< 10). The ESC coefficient is +1.13 (p = 0.33). At the session frequency the oil signal from the posts is swamped by the other oil news of the day, unlike in the two-hour windows (where the effect was −1.3%, p < 0.001).
**Reduced form (DEESC session vs none).** Korean Air +0.05% (p = 0.94), Jin Air −0.20% (p = 0.73), Jeju Air −0.53% (p = 0.45), KOSPI −0.32% (p = 0.05, wrong sign for a rally); ESC: no effect (all p ≥ 0.67). **Chip placebo:** Korean chip factor +1.24% (p = 0.28) and ESC −0.45% (p = 0.71): not significant, as expected.
**2SLS (WTI session change instrumented by DEESC).** Korean Air −0.04 (se 0.54, p = 0.94); Jin Air +0.16 (se 0.55); Jeju Air +0.43 (se 0.76); KOSPI +0.26 (se 0.25). The standard errors are too large to distinguish an effect of the size found before (−0.1 per 1% of oil) from zero; the weak first stage makes the 2SLS unreliable.
**OLS for comparison (same-window WTI change, with the same controls):** Korean Air −0.34 (p < 0.001), Jin Air −0.24, Jeju Air −0.24 (p < 0.001), KOSPI −0.00. These contemporaneous coefficients (about three times those obtained with the previous-session WTI) reflect that the WTI window is the same time as the Korean session, so both respond to the same news within the window; they are not causal.
**Reading.** The de-escalation posts are a strong oil instrument in a two-hour window but a weak one at the daily frequency in which Korean returns are available, so the oil effect on Korean airlines cannot be identified from them with these data. The only direct evidence of the posts on Korean airlines (reduced form) is null. A hourly Korean-stock series for the Korean trading hours could use the posts that appeared during Korean sessions (00:00–06:30 UTC), but few such posts exist.
