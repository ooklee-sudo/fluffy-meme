# U.S. industry stock returns around the 2026 Iran war (plan `ANALYSIS_PLAN.md`; `event_study.py`; `results_events.json`)
Fama-French 48 value-weighted industry portfolios, daily, through 31 Aug 2026. Event day 0 = Monday 2 March 2026. Market model (excess returns on Mkt-RF) estimated on the 230 trading days from 2025-02-14 to 2026-01-14 (days −260 to −31). Empirical p = share of rolling windows of the same length within the estimation period with |CAR| at least as large.
**Market.** Excess market return: +0.13% on day 0; −2.0% over days 0–4; −7.7% over days 0–19; recovered afterwards (+10.3% cumulative from day 0 through 31 Aug). Daily 0–4: +0.13, −0.98, +0.82, −0.65, −1.36.
**Pre-specified primary hypotheses (CAR[0,4], market model; three-factor in brackets).**
- H_S1 Oil and gas: **+3.6%** (+4.6%), empirical p = 0.22 — not significant at the pre-specified horizon. Day 0 alone +2.1% (p = 0.10); **over 20 days +20.4% (p < 0.001)** (post-hoc horizon).
- H_S2 Transportation (airlines, rail, trucking, shipping): −1.9% (−1.3%), p = 0.21 — right sign, not significant; 20 days −1.3% (p = 0.59).
- H_S3 Restaurants/hotels/motels ("Meals"): −1.6% (−1.0%), p = 0.33; day 0 alone −2.0% (p = 0.03); 20 days −3.5% (p = 0.26).
None of the three passes at CAR[0,4] (with a Holm correction none would pass). Signs are as predicted for all three.
**Secondary (descriptive, 48 industries; 17 of 48 with p < 0.05 at [0,4], more than the 2–3 expected by chance).** Largest negative abnormal returns over days 0–4: Gold −12.9%, Mines −11.9%, Textiles −9.1%, Tobacco −8.2%, Machinery −7.8%, Household goods −7.2%, Soda −6.9%, Electrical equipment −6.9%; positive: Books +4.1%, Business services +4.0%, Defense (Guns) +3.8%, Oil +3.6%, Entertainment (Fun) +2.6%, Telecom +2.0%. Over 20 days: Coal +25.9%, Oil +20.4%, Books +9.4%, Computers +6.2% versus Gold −29.5%, Mines −15.6%, Household goods −13.3%, Tobacco −10.6%, Soda −9.6%, Aerospace −9.1%, Food −7.2%. The pattern is sector rotation toward energy/defense/software and away from materials, gold, staples (soda, tobacco, food, household) and cyclical industrials.
**Reading.** Markets priced the war as an energy and defense gain and a loss for staples and materials; restaurants/hotels fell on day 0 (−2.0%, p = 0.03) and transport fell only modestly (−1.9% in a week, not significant). The market as a whole fell about 2% in the first week and 8% in the first month, then recovered fully by August. The marked drop in the consumer staples/tobacco/soda/household industries is not mirrored by the retail and tobacco data (`../tobacco/RESULTS.md`), so stock reactions should not be read as realized sales effects. Cumulative abnormal returns beyond about 20 days (not reported in detail) are unreliable as causal estimates. Only industry portfolios (not single stocks) were available.
