# Korea's exposure to oil-price changes: do Korean/Asian stocks and the won react more to oil than the U.S. market? (plan fixed before computing; descriptive/exploratory)

**Status.** Written 2026-10-14 after the author's statement that Korea imports almost all its oil from the Middle East and is therefore immediately hurt by higher oil prices. Facts from news (not verified against official customs data): Korea imports essentially all crude; the Middle East share was about 69–70% in early 2026 (Saudi Arabia 34%, UAE 11%, Iraq 10%, Kuwait 8.5%), over 95% of it through Hormuz, falling to 48.5% in May–July 2026 per KNOC; government and private reserves about 200 days; alternative supplies arranged. The Middle East share is high but not "almost all".
**Data.** Daily closes (local dates) of KOSPI, KOSDAQ, Samsung, SK hynix, Nikkei 225, TAIEX, S&P 500, USD/KRW (Yahoo, as in `../korea/raw/prices.csv` and `raw/prices.csv`), and WTI crude spot (FRED DCOILWTICO, through 6 Oct 2026).
**Model.** For each local date t: r_t = a + b·Δln(WTI)_prev(t) + c·r_S&P,prev(t) + e_t, where prev(t) is the latest U.S. session strictly before t (oil settles in New York time); OLS with HAC (5 lags); estimated for 2025-01-02..2026-02-27 (pre) and 2026-03-02..2026-10-06 (war); interaction test for the change in b. For the won, the dependent variable is Δln(USD/KRW) (positive = won weakens). Hypotheses (directional, stated now): b < 0 for equities and b > 0 for the won (oil up → stocks down, won weaker); Korea's |b| larger than Japan's, Taiwan's and the U.S.'s; the sensitivity larger in the war period than before.
**Limitations.** Oil and equities respond to the same war news (not exogenous), the oil move on a day is the U.S. settlement and may mix other information; Korea's sectors (chips) dominate KOSPI; the pre-war sample has small oil variance; no fuel-price data for Korean consumers.
