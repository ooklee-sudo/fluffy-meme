# Korea's sensitivity to oil prices vs other markets (plan `ANALYSIS_PLAN_OIL.md`; `analysis_oil.py`; `results_oil.json`; exploratory)
WTI: $66.96 (27 Feb) → peak $114.58 (7 Apr) → $96.24 (6 Oct); daily volatility 2.0% pre-war → 4.5% since. Local daily returns on the previous U.S. session's WTI change (coefficient per 1% WTI), with the previous S&P 500 return as control; HAC 5 lags; pre = Jan 2025–Feb 2026, war = 2 Mar–6 Oct 2026.
- **KOSPI:** with the S&P control, oil's coefficient is +0.03 (pre) and +0.09 (war, p = 0.31): no extra effect of oil beyond the U.S. market; oil alone (no control) in the war period −0.09 (p = 0.22). KOSDAQ −0.083 (p = 0.10). Samsung −0.04 (p = 0.66), SK hynix −0.10 (p = 0.36).
- **Other Asian markets, oil alone in the war period:** Nikkei −0.077 (p = 0.05), TAIEX −0.088 (p = 0.04), i.e., about the same as Korea. Korea is not more oil-sensitive than Japan or Taiwan in daily data. The change in the oil coefficient between pre-war and war is not significant for any equity series (p ≥ 0.11).
- **Won (USD/KRW, positive = won weaker):** +0.043 per 1% WTI before the war (p = 0.03), +0.024 with the S&P control in the war period (p = 0.04), +0.051 oil-only (p < 0.001): the one robust oil linkage: oil up → won weaker, present before the war and unchanged. Yet since 27 Feb the won strengthened 6.6% cumulatively despite oil's +44%, so other flows (chip exports, foreign inflows) dominated the level.
**Claim check.** Korea imports essentially all its crude; the Middle East share was about 69–70% early in 2026 (not "almost all"), falling to 48.5% in May–July as alternatives were arranged; reserves of about 200 days. Stock-market evidence: Korea's equities collapsed on the war onset (KOSPI −19% to 31 March) but the daily link to oil moves is no stronger than Japan's or Taiwan's, and the won reacts to oil as before the war. Immediate physical shortage was avoided by reserves and re-sourcing; the immediate hit was financial (equities) and cost-of-living (fuel prices), which these data cannot measure.
