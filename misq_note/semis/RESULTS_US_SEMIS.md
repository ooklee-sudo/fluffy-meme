# U.S. semiconductors and Nvidia since the war (exploratory, requested by the author; `analysis_us_semis.py`, `results_us_semis.json`)
Closes from Yahoo Finance to 13 Oct 2026; index 27 Feb 2026 = 100.
**Path.** SOX: 88.2 (30 Mar trough) → 98.8 (7 Apr) → 158.4 (29 May) → **180.7 (22 Jun peak)** → 166.5 (23 Jun) → 142.7 (27 Jul) → 139.7 (31 Jul) → 155.9 (30 Sep) → 150.8 (13 Oct). The S&P 500 was 113.5 on 13 Oct. Nvidia: 93.2 (30 Mar) → 119.2 (29 May) → 117.8 (22 Jun) → 110.9 (27 Jul) → 129.0 (13 Oct; peak 6 Oct, only −4.5% below). Micron 78.0 → 293.8 (25 Jun peak) → 199.6 (31 Jul); Intel 90.3 → 309.0 (22 Jun) → 201.0 (27 Jul); AMD 257.8 (29 May) → 302.4 (13 Oct); Broadcom peaked 2 June (+48%) and is 25.8% below it. Drawdown from the peak to the 29 July trough: SOX −28.6%, Micron −39.1%, Intel −41.9%, Broadcom −29.5% (trough 15 Sep), while Nvidia fell at most 5.4% and TSMC ADR 8.5%.
**Nvidia moved far less than the group.** On the SOX's worst days Nvidia fell less: 23 Jun SOX −8.2%, Nvidia −4.2%, Micron −14.1%, Intel −6.3%, TSMC −6.9%, AMD −5.9%, Qualcomm −8.3%; 29 Jul SOX −5.5%, Nvidia −3.6%, Micron −10.5%; 5 Jun SOX −10.8%, Nvidia −6.4%. Nvidia's own largest moves (|r| ≥ 4.3%): +5.4% 31 Mar, −4.7% 30 Apr, +5.6% 6 May, −6.4% 5 Jun, −5.1% 27 Jul, +8.4% 27 Aug.
**Largest SOX days since 2 March:** +6.1% 31 Mar, +6.1% 8 Apr (WTI −17.5%: the ceasefire), −10.8% 5 Jun, +7.6% 11 Jun (strike cancelled), +6.2% 18 Jun, −8.2% 23 Jun, −6.5% 1 Jul, +7.9% 30 Jul, +6.3% 4 Aug (WTI −5.8%), −6.0% 14 Sep. Four of ten coincide with large S&P moves or oil moves tied to war news (31 Mar, 8 Apr, 11 Jun, 23 Jun). Daily correlation with the S&P 500: SOX 0.71, Nvidia 0.66; with same-day WTI changes: SOX −0.24, Nvidia −0.17.
**Timing of the 22–23 June episode (relevant to the Korean crash).** On 22 Jun (U.S. close) the SOX was +2.0% (Micron +6.6%, Intel +5.1%, Broadcom −4.8%). The Korean chips then fell about 13% in the Korean session of 23 June (before the U.S. opened), and the U.S. followed on 23 June with SOX −8.2%. So the shock arrived after the U.S. close of 22 June and was priced first in Asia; the Korean fall was not a response to a U.S. price drop. The Trump 'hit Iran very hard again' statement (20–22 June) is a candidate trigger, but I did not identify the news or its time. In late July a similar sequence appears: Nvidia −5.1% on 27 Jul, then Korean chips −15% on 28 Jul, then the U.S. SOX −4.6% on 28 Jul and −5.5% on 29 Jul.
**Reading.** The semiconductor boom of 2026 and its two big drawdowns (late June to July) were concentrated in memory (Micron), Intel, AMD and Broadcom; Nvidia and TSMC were relatively stable. Co-movement with the S&P 500 is strong, with oil weak. The Korean chips lead or amplify these drawdowns (time-zone timing), not the other way round.
