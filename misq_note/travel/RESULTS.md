# U.S. citizens' travel abroad around the 2026 Iran war (NTTO; plan `ANALYSIS_PLAN.md`; script `analysis_travel.py`; output `results_travel.json`)
Data: NTTO U.S.-citizen departures by destination region, monthly, 1996–June 2026 (released 2 Sept 2026). Method: rolling-origin deviation D_Y (mean log gap vs forecast from the preceding 36 months), Post = March–June; ten reference years (2026 + 2012–2019, 2025). The Middle East YoY changes had been glimpsed before the plan (disclosed in the plan).

**T1 supported: Middle East.** D_2026 = **−0.468 (−37%)**, the most negative of the ten years (placebo range −0.095 to +0.057); low-tail p = 0.10 (the minimum attainable). Same with 24-month training −40%, 48-month −38%, no trend −37%, March–May only −44%. Pre-war check (Jan–Feb 2026): +7% (no pre-existing drop; within placebo range).
**T2 (spillover) not supported.** Overseas excluding Middle East −4.2% (perm p = 0.30), Europe −6.6% (p = 0.5), Asia −3.1% (p = 0.6); signs are negative but within the placebo range and not robust to the model: with no trend they are +9%, +8%, +28%; with 48-month training −9%, −9.5%, −16%.
**T3 (substitution, direction ≥ 0) holds** for Caribbean +0.6%, Mexico total +0.7%, Canada total +0.5%.
**Exploratory (not predicted):** Mexico by air −12.6% (low-tail p = 0.12, n = 8 usable years) while Mexico total (air + land) is flat, i.e., air travel to Mexico fell and land travel did not; Canada air −3.7% (p = 0.75). Non-war regions: Africa −4.7%, Central America −5.4% (p = 0.8, 0.3).

**Reading.** Americans' departures to the Middle East fell by roughly 37% in March–June 2026 relative to trend, an effect far outside past variation, consistent with the war. There is no robust evidence of an effect on travel to other regions through June 2026 (Total overseas −5.9%, p = 0.30, within normal variation). Counts are APIS non-stop air departures; connections via Europe are counted in Europe. This is a direct war effect, not a fuel-price/income effect: it cannot support the lower-income-spending hypothesis.
