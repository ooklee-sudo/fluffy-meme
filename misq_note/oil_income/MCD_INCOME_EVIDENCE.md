# McDonald's: what the company itself says about low-income traffic (collected 2026-10-14; qualitative, not pre-registered)

**Primary documents (SEC 8-K Exhibit 99.1, 2022Q1–2026Q2, searched programmatically).** The earnings press releases never use the words "low-income" or "income cohort". They do report U.S. comparable guest counts qualitatively: e.g. 2024Q2 "negative comparable guest counts" (U.S. comps −0.7%), 2024Q3 "slightly negative comparable guest counts" (+0.3%), 2025Q1 "negative comparable guest counts" (−3.6%), 2026Q2 "positive check growth ... partly offset by negative comparable guest counts" (+0.8%). In 2022–2023 U.S. releases cite "positive guest counts".
**Earnings calls (not SEC filings; read here via secondary sources, not a verbatim transcript for Q1).**
- Q1 2026 call (7 May 2026), as reported by AP, Fortune and trade press (not verified against the transcript): the CEO said the low-income consumer was still declining, partly lapping prior-year trends and partly due to high gas prices; visits by households with incomes of about $45,000 or less were still declining, while higher-income consumers showed growth and share gains; the CFO said higher gas prices "are not going to be helpful", particularly for lower-income consumers. U.S. comps were +3.9%; April same-store sales were lower in the U.S., attributed partly to tough comparisons.
- Q2 2026 call (August 2026), from an Investing.com transcript (partial; the page was truncated): the CFO said QSR industry traffic in several of the largest markets "continue to be flat to negative"; value-execution factors "accounted for about two-thirds of the customer traffic underperformance"; U.S. comps "were slightly negative in July"; the CEO: "We simply didn't execute at the level we needed to in the second quarter", only about 60–65% of the system executed the recommended pricing and the "10 items for under three bucks has not delivered against our expectation". The passages retrieved contain no income-cohort or gas-price statement.

**Implications for the proposition "low-income consumers visit fast-food chains less after the oil shock".**
1. The company's own statements (Q1 call) support a decline among low-income customers, but they say it is partly a continuation of 2025 trends ("lapping"), so it is not attributable to the war alone.
2. Two-thirds of the Q2 traffic shortfall is attributed by management to its own value-menu execution, not to income pressure; the pooled chain data show no deterioration in other chains.
3. Company statements are not independent evidence (executives have incentives to point to external causes) and income-specific traffic numbers were not disclosed in the retrieved sources. The sources of the quotes above are secondary except for the Investing.com transcript text, which is itself a vendor transcript.
