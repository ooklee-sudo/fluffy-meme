# 왜 패스트푸드 체인인가: 본문용 근거와 참고문헌 (2026-10-14)

**중요한 주의.** 아래 근거를 조사한 결과, 논리의 두 축 중 하나는 지지되고 하나는 **지지되지 않는다.**
1. 지지됨: 저소득층은 유가 급등의 부담을 더 크게 받는다.
2. **지지되지 않음: "저소득층이 패스트푸드를 더 자주 이용한다."** 미국 CDC(NCHS)에 따르면 하루 중 패스트푸드를 먹는 성인 비율은 저소득 31.7%, 중간소득 36.4%, 고소득 42.0%로 저소득층이 오히려 낮다(2013–2016). USDA ERS는 고소득 가구가 외식을 더 자주 한다고 보고한다(주당 5.5회 vs 4.2회).
따라서 본문에는 "저소득층이 더 많이 이용한다"고 쓰지 않고, **패스트푸드가 저소득층의 예산 압박이 가장 직접적으로 관측되고 측정 가능한 외식 부문**이라는 논리로 쓴다(아래 초안). 이 표현은 위 증거와 충돌하지 않는다.

## 본문 초안 (영어)
> **Why fast-food chains.** Fuel-price shocks fall disproportionately on lower-income households. Energy goods account for roughly 4 percent of outlays in the bottom income decile but about 1.5 percent in the top decile (Bank of America estimate, reported by Reuters via Global Banking & Finance 2026), and the spending of lower-income households responds most strongly to changes in gasoline prices (Gelman et al. 2023); households also adjust their grocery purchases when gasoline prices rise (Gicheva, Hastings, and Villas-Boas 2010). During the 2026 war-related price spike, a New York Fed analysis reported that households earning less than $40,000 cut gasoline consumption by 7 percent while spending 12 percent more at the pump in March (Fortune 2026).
> We focus on fast-food chains because they are the segment of the restaurant industry in which this budget pressure should be most directly observable and measurable: they are the lowest-price segment, whose demand is price-inelastic relative to sit-down dining (Okrent and Alston 2012), and listed chains publish comparable sales every quarter. Executives of the largest chain have reported traffic declines among lower-income customers of nearly ten percent for about two years (NBC News 2025; Restaurant Dive 2025) and, in 2026, attributed a weak start to the second quarter to fuel-price pressure on lower-income consumers (US News 2026).
> We do not claim that low-income households are the heaviest users of fast food. National survey data show that lower-income adults were less likely than higher-income adults to eat fast food on a given day (31.7 versus 42.0 percent; NCHS 2018), and higher-income households eat out more often overall (USDA ERS 2018). Theory is therefore ambiguous: lower disposable income can reduce visits to fast-food chains, or it can induce trade-down from full-service restaurants, as was suggested for earlier downturns (Koh, Lee, and Choi 2013). Which effect dominates is the empirical question of this paper.
