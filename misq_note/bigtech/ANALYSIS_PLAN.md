# Big-tech stock reactions to Trump's Iran threats and call-offs, 2026: analysis plan (fixed before any individual-stock return is examined)

**Status.** Written 2026-10-14 at the author's request ("big tech 8 moved significantly every time Trump threatened to attack"). Disclosure: search snippets seen before this plan reported that on 11 June 2026 (call-off of announced strikes) the Nasdaq Composite rose 2.54% and the S&P 500 1.75%, and that on 8 July the Nasdaq opened −0.2%; no individual stock return was examined. The event list below was assembled from news timelines (Wikipedia 'Iran–United States negotiations' to mid-June; armedconflicts.org for late June; CNBC/Al Jazeera for 10–11 June; trade press for 8 July and October), **not from stock prices**, but it is incomplete (the sources omit some Trump posts, and CNN counted dozens of "imminent deal" claims); selection of events remains a limitation.
**Stocks ("big tech 8", my definition; the author did not specify).** Apple, Microsoft, Alphabet (GOOGL), Amazon, Meta, Nvidia, Tesla, Broadcom. Benchmarks: S&P 500 (^GSPC) and Nasdaq Composite (^IXIC). Daily adjusted closes from Yahoo Finance (chart API), 2 Jan 2025–13 Oct 2026.
**Events (statement date → type → reaction day 0).** 2026-03-06 threat ("no deal ... unconditional surrender"); 03-23 call-off (postponed Hormuz ultimatum); 04-05 threat (power plants and bridges; Sunday → 04-06); 04-07 threat ("a whole civilization will die tonight"); 04-13 threat (naval blockade); 04-25 breakdown (cancelled envoy trip; Saturday → 04-27); 05-24 deal (memorandum "close"; Sunday; Monday 25 May is Memorial Day → 05-26); 06-10 threat (strike "very hard tonight"); 06-11 call-off (strikes cancelled, peace deal touted); 06-17 deal (signed memorandum); 06-20 threat ("hit Iran very hard again"; Saturday → 06-22); 07-08 breakdown (said memorandum "over"); 10-05 threat ("easy way or the hard way"); 10-08 call-off (ruled out a strike before the midterms). Types: N = threat/breakdown (expected negative), P = call-off/deal (expected positive). Statement times are not known precisely, so the reaction window is days 0 and +1.
**Method.** Market model for each stock on S&P 500 daily returns estimated on 3 Mar 2025–30 Jan 2026; abnormal return AR_it = r_it − (a_i + b_i r_mt). CAR_i,e = AR on day 0 + day 1. Portfolio = equal-weighted mean of the eight stocks.
**Tests.** (T1) Magnitude (the author's claim): mean over events and stocks of |CAR| against a permutation distribution built from random pairs of consecutive trading days between 2 Mar and 13 Oct 2026, excluding event days and the day after (5,000 draws); also the share of events with |portfolio CAR| larger than the 90th percentile of the same random pairs. (T2) Direction: mean of the portfolio CAR on N events (expected < 0) and on P events (expected > 0), each against the same random-pair distribution. (T3) Descriptive: table by event of raw and abnormal returns for the portfolio, Nasdaq and each stock.
**Interpretation rules.** The "every time" claim is supported only if a large majority of the 14 events have portfolio |CAR| above the random-pair 90th percentile and T1 p < 0.05. Because events are news-selected, a significant result can partly reflect that days with news are days with larger moves in general.
**Limitations.** Incomplete event list; unknown statement times; confounding news on the same days (earnings, Fed, tariffs); the market model on the S&P 500 removes most market-wide variation; eight highly correlated stocks (not independent observations).
