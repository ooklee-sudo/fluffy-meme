We submit "Early Adoption, Delayed Displacement? Generative Visual AI and Creative Work" for consideration as a Research Article in Information Systems Research.

The paper asks whether generative AI that creates images and video is displacing creative workers or whether its effects are still to come. We propose a model that separates adoption from displacement: the change in labor demand in an occupation is the share of employers that have adopted times the net effect among adopters, where adoption requires acceptable output quality at low review cost and is limited by frictions in expertise and legal clarity. We then confront the model with open data from three sources: Eurostat's enterprise survey on AI (adoption and reasons for non-adoption), online job-advert and vacancy statistics for the United Kingdom, 16 European countries, and Canada, and an LLM-coded measure of occupational exposure to visual content creation, which we validate with two independent human coders.

The main findings are that adoption is already substantial (9.6% of European firms with ten or more employees use AI that generates pictures, video, or audio, and about 36% in publishing, film, and broadcasting) and that firms cite lack of expertise and legal and privacy uncertainty far more than lack of usefulness; that exposure to visual content creation is associated with small and fragile declines in hiring after 2022 that we cannot separate from a pre-existing trend; and that the largest observed declines are too large for current adoption levels to explain through substitution among adopters. We also use the model to show when a given effect among adopters would become detectable in data of this kind.

The paper speaks to the information systems literature on diffusion and assimilation gaps, and on complementary assets in the adoption of general-purpose technologies. We report plainly where the evidence is weak: identification relies on aggregate data, and agreement between human coders on the exposure construct is low.

This manuscript is not under review elsewhere. All data are public and the replication materials are available in an anonymized repository. [Add: suggested department editor or area, suggested reviewers, and any disclosures required by the journal, including the generative-AI statement.]
