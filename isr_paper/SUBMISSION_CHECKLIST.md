## 4. Content risks reviewers are likely to raise (all are stated in the manuscript's limitations)
1. Identification: exposure is a pre-determined continuous measure, but the pre-period contains COVID and the post-2022 pattern resembles a reversal of the 2022 recovery; estimates are small and rest on a few occupations.
2. Measurement validity: two human coders agree poorly with each other (weighted kappa 0.18) and with the narrow LLM score; the broad score agrees moderately (0.31 to 0.41) and its rubric was shaped by one coder.
3. Aggregate data: no firm-level link between adoption and hiring; adoption is observed in European surveys, hiring in UK, European, and Canadian data.
4. The projection is a scenario analysis, not a forecast.
5. Positioning in IS literature: the manuscript cites Fichman and Kemerer (1999) and recent platform studies in other journals; reviewers at ISR will expect more recent ISR, MISQ, and JMIS work on AI adoption, assimilation, and creative work. Add 5 to 10 verified references.
