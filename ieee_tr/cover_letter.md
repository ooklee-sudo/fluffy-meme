Dear Editor,

Please consider the enclosed manuscript, "Condition-Based Maintenance of Large Language Models Under Knowledge Drift and Imperfect Repair," for publication in the IEEE Transactions on Reliability.

A deployed language model deteriorates: the world drifts away from its training data, and the usual remedy, a parameter update, can itself damage competence that users depend on. This is the structure of an imperfect-repair maintenance problem, and the manuscript treats it as one. Staleness is modeled as a shock-and-wear process driven by a marked point process, forgetting as an imperfect repair with a Fisher-information-weighted damage term, and the choice among repair mechanisms as an average-cost Markov decision process.

The main results are (i) a square-root law linking update cost, drift intensity, and the optimal maintenance interval; (ii) a comparative static showing that a repair leaving less residual damage should be applied more often; (iii) a nested control-limit theorem for a graduated repair menu; and (iv) a closed-form test for when an external retrieval store, which compensates for drift without repairing the parameters, is worth its upkeep. A measurement study on a public 70-million-parameter checkpoint supplies the model inputs and shows that the assumed ordering of repair actions has to be verified from measurements and cannot be read off a mechanism's name.

We believe the paper fits the journal's maintenance and imperfect-repair readership because it extends that theory to a new class of asset whose repair technology is gradient-based, and because it yields testable predictions for practitioners who now operate such assets.

The manuscript is original, is not under consideration elsewhere, and has been approved by the author. The author declares no competing interests.
