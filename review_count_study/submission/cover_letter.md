Dear Editor,

I submit the manuscript "Do disagreements between LLM-inferred and actual star ratings predict review helpfulness? A replication and extension" for consideration in the Replication Corner of *Marketing Letters*.

**What I replicate.** Winter, Zacharias, and Hartwig (2026), published in *Marketing Letters*, propose that the gap between a large language model's inferred star rating and the rating a reviewer actually gave predicts lower review helpfulness, particularly for experience goods. Because the approach needs no training data, it is of practical interest to platforms. I examine whether this finding holds in a different, publicly available data source and with different analytic choices.

**What I do.** Using 25,783 Amazon reviews from four product categories, I (i) re-estimate the original Tobit specification and add count models (negative binomial, with a zero-inflation diagnostic); (ii) run three LLMs from different families, each with our own prompt and with the system prompt reported in the original supplementary material; and (iii) replace the category-level search/experience and hedonic/utilitarian assumptions with product-level labels for 20,670 products, which we validate against two independent human coders on a random sample (κ = .82 and .76).

**What I find.** The LLMs recover authors' ratings closely. Larger gaps do not predict fewer helpful votes in my observational data; the association is positive across all six LLM–prompt combinations. The positive coefficient for search goods agrees with the original Model 2 (and with the original authors' GPT-5-nano check), whereas the stronger penalty for experience goods is not significant. Hedonic goods show smaller gaps only when goods are classified at the product level. I do not replicate the original experiment and therefore make no claim about the proposed mechanism.

**Fit with the Replication Corner.** The manuscript is about 3,040 words including abstract and references, has three tables, and is accompanied by an online appendix (about 1,540 words) and a replication package with code, prompts, identifiers, LLM outputs, and human validation labels. Raw review text is not redistributed because of the data provider's terms.

**Transparency.** The study was not preregistered; I analyzed a 2,000-review pilot first, and I report this and all models estimated for the main question. I used the original authors' openly available supplementary materials, including their prompt, and did not contact them. Generative AI was used as described in the manuscript and on the title page.
