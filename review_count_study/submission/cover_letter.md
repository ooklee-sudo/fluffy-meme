Dear Editor,

I submit the manuscript "Does disagreement between LLM-inferred and actual star ratings predict review helpfulness? Evidence from three language models and 26,000 Amazon reviews" as a regular article for *Marketing Letters*.

**Question.** Winter, Zacharias, and Hartwig (2026), published in *Marketing Letters*, propose that the gap between a large language model's inferred star rating and the rating a reviewer actually gave predicts lower review helpfulness, particularly for experience goods. Because the measure needs no training data, platforms could use it. I ask whether the claim holds in an independent, public data source, and what the gap captures.

**Approach.** Using 25,783 Amazon reviews from four categories, I re-estimate the original Tobit specification and add negative binomial models for the helpful-vote counts (with a zero-inflation diagnostic); run three LLMs from different families, each with my own prompt and with the system prompt in the original supplementary material; and replace the category-level goods assumptions with product-level labels for 20,670 products, validated against two human coders on 100 products and used in a misclassification sensitivity analysis.

**Findings.** The LLMs recover authors' ratings closely (r = .89–.92). Larger gaps are associated with more, not fewer, helpful votes in all six LLM–prompt combinations, and the extra penalty for experience goods is not significant. Hedonic goods show smaller gaps only under product-level labels. In exploratory analyses the positive association is concentrated in reviews whose text is read as more critical than the star rating and is not found when the text is read as more positive. This suggests that the gap partly reflects informative critical text rather than inconsistency. I do not replicate the original experiment and make no claim about its mechanism.

**Contribution and transparency.** The manuscript contributes (i) an independent test of a published claim with count models, three LLMs, and the original prompt; (ii) human-validated product-level goods labels with a sensitivity analysis for label error; and (iii) a direction-of-disagreement analysis that qualifies how the gap should be interpreted. The study was not preregistered and followed a pilot; the direction and rating-level analyses and the alternative explanation behind them were developed after the main results and are labeled exploratory. Earlier exploratory work on LLM-coded content features (two small unreported samples) is disclosed in the manuscript. I used the original authors' openly available supplementary materials, including their prompt, and did not contact them. Generative AI was used as described in the manuscript and on the title page.

**Materials.** The manuscript is about 4,950 words including abstract, references, and tables (4 tables); the online appendix is about 1,980 words including tables. The replication package contains code, prompts, identifiers, ratings, LLM outputs, and human validation labels; raw review text is not redistributed.
